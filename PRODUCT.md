# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who want knowledge that keeps working when the network does not: students and
teachers on poor connections, researchers, travellers, hill walkers and mountaineers,
engineers, people preparing for emergencies, and anyone who keeps a personal reference
library.

They install Outpost once on a laptop, a home server, a NAS or a Raspberry Pi. Then they
use it from whatever screen is nearby: a desktop at a desk, a phone in a tent, a tablet
in a workshop. Sessions are task-shaped. Someone is looking something up, reading,
writing a note, checking a map, or tending the library (downloading, moving, verifying).

The person who installs Outpost also looks after it, but should never feel like they
are running a server. Storage, downloads and settings are part of the same calm product
as reading and writing.

## Product Purpose

Outpost is a self-hosted, offline-first knowledge platform. It brings together an
offline encyclopedia, a document library, a personal notes workspace, offline maps and
one search box across all of it.

Success is when a person can pull the network cable and not notice: everything they
chose to keep is still there, findable in a few keystrokes, readable, and editable.

## Brand Personality

Calm, capable, trustworthy.

The voice is plain and exact. It says what a thing is and what will happen next. It
never sells, never alarms, and never hides a failure. Downloads, storage and integrity
checks are stated in real numbers. When something is unavailable because the device is
offline, the interface says so directly and says what still works.

The emotional goal is quiet confidence: the feeling of a well-kept field station where
everything has a place and the lamp is on.

## Anti-references

- Server administration panels: dense grey tables, status LEDs, config-file vocabulary,
  tabs of raw settings.
- "Prepper" aesthetics: camouflage, hazard stripes, distressed stencil type, alarmism.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: hero metrics, identical card grids, gradient decoration.
- A Notion clone. The calm and the page model are an inspiration; the branding, icons
  and layout details are not to be copied.

## Design Principles

1. **Works when nothing else does.** Every asset is local. No screen depends on the
   internet to render, and the interface is honest about what needs a connection.
2. **The tool disappears into the task.** Reading, writing and finding come first.
   Chrome is quiet, familiar and consistent from screen to screen.
3. **Say exactly what is true.** Sizes, free space, progress, checksums and failures are
   shown as they are. No placeholder actions, no invented data.
4. **Legible anywhere.** Text must read well on a phone at arm's length, in a dim tent,
   and on a cheap panel. Legibility decides type, contrast and density.
5. **Never surprise someone about their files.** Nothing is moved, overwritten or
   deleted without the person asking for it and seeing what will happen.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI boundaries.
- Fully keyboard operable, visible focus on every interactive element, logical order.
- Reduced motion respected everywhere.
- Colour is never the only carrier of state; status always has text or an icon.
- Touch targets at least 44px on mobile layouts.
- Works at 200% zoom and from 320px wide.
