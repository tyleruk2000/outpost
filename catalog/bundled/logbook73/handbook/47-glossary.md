# Glossary

Radio has a vocabulary all of its own, and half the battle of being a beginner is that every conversation, menu and magazine assumes you already speak it. This glossary defines every recurring term in this handbook in a sentence or three, grouped alphabetically. Where a term has a home chapter, the link takes you to the full story; use this page as the quick reminder, not the lesson.

> Hunting one word rather than reading down the list? The searchable glossary has a live filter and orders the terms by when you tend to meet them on the air.

## 0–9

**73** — The traditional sign-off meaning "best regards", inherited from telegraph shorthand. Sent at the end of nearly every contact, spoken or keyed: "Thanks for the QSO, 73!" See [Amateur Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amateur-radio).

## A

**AGC** — Automatic Gain Control: a receiver circuit that automatically reduces gain on strong signals and raises it on weak ones, so loud stations don't deafen you and quiet ones stay audible. See [Receivers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/receivers).

**ALC** — Automatic Level Control: a transmitter circuit that holds output power at a safe maximum by throttling drive, preventing an over-enthusiastic microphone setting from producing a distorted, splattering signal. See [Transmitters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/transmitters).

**AM** — Amplitude Modulation: carrying information by varying the strength of a carrier wave. Used by medium-wave broadcasters and aviation. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**Amateur radio** — The licensed hobby and service of experimenting and communicating by radio; also called ham radio. See [Amateur Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amateur-radio).

**Amplifier** — A circuit that makes a signal larger. Varieties include small-signal, power (PA) and low-noise (LNA) amplifiers. See [Amplifiers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amplifiers).

**Antenna** — The transducer that converts electrical signals into radio waves and back. The single most cost-effective part of any station. See [Antennas](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/antennas).

**APRS** — Automatic Packet Reporting System: a packet-radio network carrying position reports, weather and short messages, mostly on VHF. See [APRS](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/aprs).

**ATU** — Antenna Tuning Unit (antenna "tuner"): a matching network between transmitter and feedline that transforms the impedance the radio sees, letting it deliver full power into an imperfect antenna. It does not fix the antenna itself. See [SWR](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/swr).

**AX.25** — The packet-radio protocol amateurs use to frame data with callsigns and error checking; the foundation of APRS and traditional packet networks. See [Packet Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/packet-radio).

## B

**Balun** — A transformer that joins a *bal*anced antenna (like a dipole) to an *un*balanced feedline (like coax), stopping stray currents flowing on the outside of the cable. See [Antennas](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/antennas).

**Band** — A range of frequencies allocated to a service, usually named by approximate wavelength: the 40 m band sits around 7 MHz (exact edges vary by ITU region). See [HF](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/hf).

**Band plan** — The voluntary agreement dividing each amateur band by mode and activity (CW here, SSB there, FT8 just so), maintained by the IARU and national societies.

**Bandwidth** — The width of spectrum a signal occupies, from ~50 Hz for FT8 to ~200 kHz for broadcast FM. Narrower bandwidth admits less noise. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**BFO** — Beat Frequency Oscillator: the receiver oscillator that re-inserts the missing carrier so SSB and CW become intelligible audio. See [Receivers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/receivers).

## C

**Callsign** — Your licensed on-air identity, issued by your national regulator; the prefix identifies the country (`M7ABC` is a UK example). See [Getting Licensed](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/getting-licensed).

**Carrier** — The steady radio-frequency sine wave that modulation reshapes to carry information. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**CAT** — Computer Aided Transceiver control: the serial/USB command interface by which software (loggers, WSJT-X) reads and sets a radio's frequency, mode and PTT.

**Coax** — Coaxial cable: shielded feedline with a centre conductor inside a screen, the standard way to connect radio to antenna. See [Transmission Lines](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/transmission-lines).

**CTCSS** — Continuous Tone-Coded Squelch System: a quiet sub-audible tone transmitted alongside speech; repeaters open only for the correct tone, keeping out noise and distant co-channel signals. See [Repeaters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/repeaters); the standard tones are listed in the CTCSS reference.

**CW** — Continuous Wave: Morse code by on-off keying of a carrier — the narrowest and oldest mode still in daily use. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation); the Morse trainer built into this app teaches it.

## D

**dB** — Decibel: the logarithmic unit for power ratios; +3 dB doubles power, +10 dB is ten times. Gains and losses in dB just add. See [Decibels](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/decibels).

**dBi** — Decibels of antenna gain relative to an isotropic (perfectly non-directional, imaginary) radiator. See [Decibels](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/decibels).

**dBm** — Decibels relative to one milliwatt: an absolute power level, so `0 dBm` = 1 mW and `30 dBm` = 1 W. See [Decibels](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/decibels).

**Digital voice** — Voice encoded as data before transmission: DMR, D-STAR and System Fusion are the main amateur flavours. See [Digital Voice](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/digital-voice).

**Dipole** — The fundamental antenna: two equal wires fed in the middle, a half-wavelength end to end. See [Antennas](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/antennas).

**D-RAP** — NOAA's D-Region Absorption Prediction (often written DRAP): a modelled map of the highest frequency losing at least 1 dB to D-layer absorption, driven by solar X-rays and proton events. Below that frequency, expect weaker signals; it is guidance, not a measurement and not a LUF. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

**Dummy load** — A shielded resistor that absorbs a transmitter's full output without radiating, for safe testing and adjustment.

**DX** — Long-distance contacts, or the distant station itself; "working DX" is contacting far-away places. See [HF](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/hf).

**DXCC** — The DX Century Club award scheme and its list of ~340 "entities" (countries and territories); the classic lifetime chase, and the country list your LogBook73 awards page counts against.

**Dynamic range** — A receiver's ability to hear weak signals in the presence of strong ones — the span between the quietest and loudest it can handle at once. See [Dynamic Range](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/dynamic-range).

## E

**Electromagnetic wave** — Travelling, self-sustaining electric and magnetic fields; light and radio are the same phenomenon at different frequencies. See [Electromagnetic Waves](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/electromagnetic-waves).

**EME** — Earth–Moon–Earth, or moonbounce: using the Moon as a passive reflector for contacts, the ultimate weak-signal path. See [Moonbounce (EME)](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/moonbounce-eme).

**ERP / EIRP** — Effective Radiated Power / Effective Isotropic Radiated Power: transmitter power adjusted for feedline loss and antenna gain — what your station effectively radiates in its best direction, referenced to a dipole (ERP) or isotropic antenna (EIRP). See [RF Power](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/rf-power).

## F

**FFT** — Fast Fourier Transform: the algorithm that converts a stretch of signal samples into a spectrum, powering every waterfall display. See [FFTs & Waterfalls](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/ffts-and-waterfalls).

**Filter** — A circuit that passes some frequencies and blocks others: low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch. See [Filters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/filters).

**FM** — Frequency Modulation: carrying information by varying a carrier's frequency; noise-resistant, used for VHF/UHF voice and broadcast radio. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**fmin** — The lowest frequency at which an ionosonde detected any echo in one sounding. It rises when D-layer absorption grows, so its trend is a useful hint, but it depends on the sounder too and is not your station's LUF. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

**foF2** — The critical frequency of the F2 layer: the highest frequency, sent straight up, that the layer returns, measured by an ionosonde. Above it, vertical (NVIS) signals pass through; it describes the sky over the sounder, not everywhere. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

**Foundation licence** — The UK's name for its entry-level amateur licence; most countries offer an equivalent entry-level class granting a callsign with reduced power. See [Getting Licensed](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/getting-licensed).

**Frequency** — How many times a wave oscillates per second, measured in hertz (Hz). See [Frequency & Wavelength](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/frequency-and-wavelength).

**FT8** — A digital mode of 15-second, computer-generated transmissions that decodes far below audibility; the most popular HF mode of the modern era. See [FT8](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/ft8).

## G

**Gain** — Amplification, in dB; for antennas, focusing of radiated power in a preferred direction (at the expense of others). See [Decibels](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/decibels).

**GNU Radio** — Free software for building radios as flowgraphs of signal-processing blocks. See [GNU Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/gnu-radio).

**Greyline (grayline)** — The twilight band between day and night on Earth, along which HF propagation is often briefly and spectacularly enhanced. See [Propagation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/propagation).

**Grid square** — The Maidenhead locator system dividing the world into lettered-and-numbered squares (e.g. `IO91`) for concise position exchange, beloved of VHF and satellite operators. See [Satellites](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/satellites).

## H

**Harmonic** — An unwanted transmitter output at an integer multiple of the operating frequency (a 7 MHz transmitter's second harmonic is 14 MHz); low-pass filters exist largely to suppress them. See [TinySA](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/tinysa).

**Hertz (Hz)** — One cycle per second, the unit of frequency; kHz, MHz and GHz are thousands, millions and billions. See [Frequency & Wavelength](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/frequency-and-wavelength).

**HF** — High Frequency, 3–30 MHz: the bands where the ionosphere enables worldwide propagation. See [HF](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/hf).

## I

**IF** — Intermediate Frequency: the fixed frequency to which a superhet receiver converts every incoming signal so filtering and amplification can be done once, well. See [Receivers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/receivers).

**Image** — A "ghost" frequency that a superhet receiver hears in addition to the wanted one, an artefact of mixing. See [Overload & Ghost Signals](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/overload-and-ghost-signals).

**IMD** — Intermodulation Distortion: false signals created when strong signals mix in a receiver's (or transmitter's) imperfect stages. See [Overload & Ghost Signals](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/overload-and-ghost-signals).

**Impedance** — The AC cousin of resistance, in ohms, describing how a load takes current relative to voltage; radio systems standardise on 50 Ω. See [Impedance](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/impedance).

**Ionogram** — An ionosonde's plot of echo (virtual) height against frequency, from which foF2, fmin and MUF(3000)F2 are read; the ionosphere's weather chart. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

**Ionosphere** — The Sun-ionised upper atmosphere layers that refract HF signals back to Earth, making long-distance shortwave contact possible. See [Propagation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/propagation).

**IQ** — In-phase and Quadrature: the two-channel representation of a signal that preserves both amplitude and phase, the native data format of every SDR. See [IQ Signals](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/iq-signals).

**ISS** — The International Space Station, which carries amateur radio for voice, packet and school contacts. See [ISS](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/iss).

## L

**LNA** — Low-Noise Amplifier: a first-stage amplifier designed to add as little noise as possible, placed close to the antenna. See [Amplifiers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amplifiers).

**LO** — Local Oscillator: the receiver- or transmitter-internal signal fed to a mixer to shift frequencies up or down. See [Mixers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/mixers).

**LoRa** — A long-range, low-power chirp modulation for licence-free bands, underpinning Meshtastic and much IoT radio. See [LoRa & Meshtastic](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/lora-and-meshtastic).

**LSB** — Lower Sideband: the SSB variant conventionally used below 10 MHz (80 m, 40 m). See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**LUF** — Lowest Usable Frequency: the lowest frequency giving a usable signal on a stated path for a stated mode, power and antenna. It depends on the station as much as the sky — FT8 has a lower LUF than SSB on the same path. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

## M

**MDS** — Minimum Discernible Signal: the weakest signal a receiver can just detect above its own noise — the hard number behind "sensitivity". See [Sensitivity](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sensitivity).

**Meshtastic** — An open-source project building encrypted text-messaging mesh networks from cheap LoRa modules. See [LoRa & Meshtastic](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/lora-and-meshtastic).

**Microwave** — Frequencies above about 3 GHz, where dishes and precision reign. See [Microwave](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/microwave).

**Mixer** — A circuit that multiplies two signals to produce sum and difference frequencies — the tool for moving signals around the spectrum. See [Mixers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/mixers).

**Modulation** — Impressing information onto a carrier by varying its amplitude, frequency or phase. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**MUF / LUF** — Maximum and Lowest Usable Frequency: the ceiling and floor of ionospheric propagation between two points at a given moment; the usable window lies between them. See [Propagation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/propagation).

**MUF(3000)** — The F2 maximum usable frequency for a standard 3,000 km single hop (formally MUF(3000)F2), scaled from an ionogram as foF2 × M(3000)F2, typically about three times foF2. A long-haul proxy, not the MUF of any particular path. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

## N

**NanoVNA** — A pocket-sized, low-cost vector network analyser used to measure antennas, SWR and cables. See [NanoVNA](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/nanovna).

**NF** — Noise Figure: how much noise a receiver or amplifier adds, in dB, compared with a theoretically perfect one. See [Sensitivity](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sensitivity).

**Noise** — Unwanted random signal from nature and electronics that sets the real limit on what you can hear; the _noise floor_ is its level on your display. See [Noise](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/noise).

**NVIS** — Near Vertical Incidence Skywave: sending HF almost straight up so it returns nearby, covering a region out to a few hundred kilometres. It needs a frequency below about foF2, which usually means 80, 60 or 40 m. See [Reading the Ionosphere](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/reading-the-ionosphere).

## O

**Ofcom** — The UK's national communications regulator. Every country has one — the FCC in the US, the BNetzA in Germany… — issuing amateur licences and setting their conditions. See [Getting Licensed](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/getting-licensed).

**Offset (repeater)** — The fixed difference between a repeater's input and output frequencies; −600 kHz on 2 m is near-universal, while 70 cm offsets vary by country (+1.6 MHz in the UK, ±5 MHz in the Americas). See [Repeaters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/repeaters).

**Oscillator** — A circuit that generates a steady signal at a chosen frequency — crystal, LC or synthesised. See [Oscillators](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/oscillators).

**Overload** — What happens when signals too strong for a receiver's front end drive it into distortion, producing ghost signals and a desensitised band. See [Overload & Ghost Signals](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/overload-and-ghost-signals).

## P

**Packet radio** — Data transmission in addressed, error-checked bursts over AX.25 — amateur radio's original wireless network. See [Packet Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/packet-radio).

**PEP** — Peak Envelope Power: transmitter power measured at the peaks of the modulation envelope; the standard way SSB power (and most licence limits) are specified. See [RF Power](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/rf-power).

**PL-259** — The classic "UHF" plug fitted to most HF gear and coax, mating with the SO-239 socket. Despite the name, best below ~150 MHz. See [Transmission Lines](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/transmission-lines).

**PLL** — Phase-Locked Loop: the feedback circuit that disciplines a tunable oscillator against a crystal reference, giving modern radios their stable, precise frequencies. See [Oscillators](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/oscillators).

**PMR446** — The European licence-free walkie-talkie allocation around 446 MHz — sixteen FM channels anyone may use; the Americas' equivalent is FRS, around 462–467 MHz.

**Propagation** — How radio waves travel from A to B: ground wave, line of sight, ionospheric skip and rarer paths. See [Propagation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/propagation).

**PTT** — Push To Talk: the switch (on microphone or via CAT) that keys the transmitter.

## Q

**QRM / QRN** — Q-code shorthand for interference: QRM is man-made (other stations, electronics), QRN is natural static. See [Noise](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/noise).

**QRP** — Low-power operating, conventionally 5 W or less; a sport of its own.

**QSL** — Confirmation of a contact, traditionally a postcard, now often electronic; "QSL?" also means "do you confirm?".

**QSO** — A two-way contact between stations — the fundamental unit of your logbook. See [Amateur Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amateur-radio).

**QTH** — Your station's location; "my QTH is Leeds".

## R

**Radio** — Communication by electromagnetic waves — the subject of this whole book, defined properly in [What Is Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/what-is-radio).

**Receiver** — The device that selects one signal from the spectrum, amplifies it and recovers its information. See [Receivers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/receivers).

**Repeater** — An automated hilltop station that receives on one frequency and simultaneously retransmits on another, extending handheld range across a region. See [Repeaters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/repeaters).

**Return loss** — SWR expressed in dB: how far the reflected power is below the forward power; bigger numbers are better matches. See [SWR](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/swr).

**RF** — Radio Frequency: signals at frequencies capable of radiating, roughly 3 kHz–300 GHz; "RF power" is transmitter output measured in watts. See [RF Power](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/rf-power).

**RST** — The Readability–Strength–Tone signal report (e.g. `599`); voice contacts use just RS (`59`). See [Amateur Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/amateur-radio).

**RTL-SDR** — The ~$30/€30 USB dongle, descended from TV tuners, that made SDR a mass hobby: a capable VHF/UHF receiver and a limited HF one. See [Software Defined Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/software-defined-radio).

## S

**Sample rate** — How many measurements per second an SDR (or sound card) takes of a signal; it sets the bandwidth you can capture and must be tracked carefully in tools like [GNU Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/gnu-radio).

**Satellite (amateur)** — Orbiting relay stations amateurs can work with modest equipment. See [Satellites](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/satellites).

**SDR** — Software Defined Radio: hardware digitises the spectrum and software does the filtering and demodulating. See [Software Defined Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/software-defined-radio).

**SDR++** — A free, cross-platform SDR receiver application, this handbook's recommended starting point. See [SDR++](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sdr-plus-plus).

**Sensitivity** — How weak a signal a receiver can usefully hear, limited by noise. See [Sensitivity](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sensitivity).

**Shack** — Affectionate name for wherever your station lives, be it a shed or a shelf. See [Building Your First Station](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/building-your-first-station).

**Simplex** — Direct station-to-station operation on a single frequency, with no repeater in between.

**SINAD** — Signal-plus-noise-and-distortion to noise-and-distortion ratio: a standard figure of merit for receiver sensitivity, especially FM. See [Sensitivity](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sensitivity).

**S-meter / S-unit** — The received-strength meter and its scale: S1 (barely there) to S9 (strong), each S-unit nominally 6 dB, with very strong signals reported as "dB over S9". See [Decibels](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/decibels).

**Smith chart** — The circular chart on which impedance and matching problems become geometry. See [Smith Charts](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/smith-charts).

**SNR** — Signal-to-Noise Ratio, in dB: how far a signal sits above the noise — the number that actually determines readability. See [Noise](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/noise).

**Squelch** — A circuit that mutes receiver audio until a signal exceeds a set threshold, sparing you the hiss between transmissions.

**SSB** — Single Sideband: AM with the carrier and one sideband removed — all power in one voice copy; the standard HF voice mode, as USB above 10 MHz and LSB below. See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**SSTV** — Slow-Scan Television: transmitting still pictures as audio tones. See [SSTV](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sstv).

**Superhet** — Superheterodyne: the receiver architecture that mixes every signal to a fixed IF; the dominant design of the analogue era. See [Receivers](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/receivers).

**SWL** — Shortwave Listener: someone who monitors and logs transmissions without transmitting — a proud tradition, and no licence needed.

**SWR / VSWR** — (Voltage) Standing Wave Ratio: how well antenna and feedline match, 1:1 being perfect; a measure of reflected power. See [SWR](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/swr).

## T

**TinySA** — A pocket-sized, low-cost spectrum analyser, ideal for checking transmitter harmonics. See [TinySA](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/tinysa).

**TNC** — Terminal Node Controller: the modem that turns data into packet-radio tones and back; nowadays often a sound card and software. See [Packet Radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/packet-radio).

**Transceiver** — A transmitter and receiver in one box — what most people mean by "a radio".

**Transmission line** — The cable (coax or ladder line) carrying RF between radio and antenna, with characteristic impedance and loss. See [Transmission Lines](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/transmission-lines).

**Transmitter** — The chain of stages that turns information into a modulated, amplified RF signal at the antenna. See [Transmitters](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/transmitters).

**Transverter** — A converter that shifts a radio's transmit _and_ receive coverage to another band — how HF rigs reach microwave bands. See [Microwave](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/microwave).

## U

**UHF** — Ultra High Frequency, 300 MHz–3 GHz: repeaters, satellites and handhelds. See [UHF](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/uhf).

**Upconverter** — A small mixer box that shifts HF signals up into the range a VHF-only SDR can tune. See [SDR++](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/sdr-plus-plus).

**USB (sideband)** — Upper Sideband: the SSB variant used above 10 MHz and for nearly all HF digital modes. (Also, confusingly, the computer connector.) See [Modulation](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/modulation).

**UTC** — Coordinated Universal Time: the single worldwide clock all logs use, so two stations' records agree regardless of time zones. LogBook73 timestamps in UTC.

## V

**VFO** — Variable Frequency Oscillator: the tunable oscillator that sets where a radio listens or transmits — in practice, "the tuning knob". See [Oscillators](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/oscillators).

**VHF** — Very High Frequency, 30–300 MHz: mostly line-of-sight, home of 2 m and local life. See [VHF](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/vhf).

**VNA** — Vector Network Analyser: an instrument measuring both magnitude and phase of reflection and transmission — what a NanoVNA is. See [NanoVNA](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/nanovna).

## W

**Waterfall** — The scrolling frequency-versus-time display that turns spectrum into a readable picture. See [FFTs & Waterfalls](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/ffts-and-waterfalls).

**Wavelength** — The physical length of one wave cycle: `λ = 300 / f(MHz)` metres. Bands are named by it. See [Frequency & Wavelength](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/frequency-and-wavelength).

**WSJT-X** — The free software behind FT8 and its sibling weak-signal modes. See [FT8](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/ft8).

## Y

**Yagi** — The classic directional beam antenna of TV-aerial fame: a driven element plus reflector and directors, focusing power one way. See [Antennas](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/antennas).

## Z

**Z** — The conventional symbol for impedance, as in `Z = 50 Ω`. If you can read that, this glossary has done its job. See [Impedance](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/impedance).

For the numbers, tables and formulas that go with these words, turn to the [Appendix](outpost:entry/logbook73.handbook/appendix).
