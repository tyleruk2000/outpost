# Best practice, legality and a radio that stays reliable

## Installation is safety work

- Mount outside knee/head impact paths and airbag deployment zones.
- Keep the heatsink ventilated and away from direct sun where practical.
- Route the supplied fused lead securely to a suitable 12 V source; protect it at chafe points.
- Disconnect the vehicle battery negative while making permanent connections, following vehicle-maker procedures for modern electronics.
- Use the specified 10 A fuses. A fuse protects wiring and fire risk; a larger one is not an upgrade.
- Never transmit without a 50 Ω antenna or dummy load.
- Never ground either side of the RT95's bridge-tied external-speaker output.

If you are not confident working around vehicle electrics, airbags or battery wiring, use a competent installer.

## The radio's band limit is not your licence

EU, US and other production versions differ. Your lawful frequencies, modes, power and identification requirements come from your national licence and band plan, not the fact that the keypad accepts a value. Stay inside edges with room for the occupied signal, and re-check any code plug obtained from another country.

## Listen, identify and use minimum necessary power

Busy lockout is a useful guard, not a substitute for listening. Leave a pause, give your callsign as required, keep overs concise and use 5 W where it is reliable. Higher power adds heat and electrical load and can worsen co-site interference.

## Tone etiquette

- Send only the access tone/code the system publishes.
- Do not use receive tones to pretend a busy shared channel is empty.
- Do not repeatedly send a 1750 Hz or other burst when the repeater does not answer.
- Keep keypad DTMF brief and purposeful.
- Leave roving experiments with 5-tone remote kill/wake off the amateur bands.

## Scrambling and “encryption”

Some RT95 descriptions call the optional voice scrambler encryption. It is an analogue obfuscation feature, not secure communications. Amateur services generally require communications to be open and not encoded to obscure meaning, subject to narrow national exceptions. Leave scrambling off for normal amateur use and never market it as privacy.

## Vehicle operation

Programme before moving. Use clear memory names, a reachable microphone, modest display brightness and a minimal key layout. Do not type frequencies, browse menus or stare at the TFT while driving. Pull over safely if the operation requires more than one obvious control.

VOX can be triggered by road noise, passengers, music or navigation prompts; it also keeps a live microphone without the deliberate action of PTT. Leave it off unless a tested accessibility or controlled hands-free setup genuinely needs it.

## RF exposure

A 25 W mobile with an external antenna can create meaningful near-field exposure. Install the antenna with appropriate separation from occupants and bystanders, follow national exposure rules and the antenna maker's guidance, and account for duty cycle. Do not test high power with someone touching or standing close to the antenna.

## Heat and duty cycle

The metal case is the heatsink. Keep vents and surfaces clear, avoid repeated long FM transmissions, and use TOT. If the case becomes unusually hot, stop transmitting and investigate antenna match, airflow, supply voltage and operating pattern.

## Keep the code plug explainable

- names should identify the actual repeater/channel, not just “local”;
- keep receive-only channels TX-inhibited;
- store source and review date outside the radio;
- remove dead repeaters from scan rather than letting the list grow forever;
- retain factory and known-good backups;
- after every write, read back and spot-check TX frequency, tone, width and permission.

## Firmware/software caution

Use official/current software or a CHIRP release that explicitly supports RT95. Do not assume a visually identical model has an interchangeable image. Never interrupt a write or cycle the radio while connected. Treat a factory reset as destructive until a backup proves otherwise.

## A six-month check

Twice a year—or before a major trip/event:

1. inspect mount, power lead, fuses, coax and antenna connector;
2. measure SWR on both bands;
3. confirm supply voltage under high-power PTT into a suitable load;
4. update repeater details from authoritative sources;
5. test low/medium/high power and both speakers;
6. export a fresh known-good backup;
7. check every receive-only memory still has TX disabled.
