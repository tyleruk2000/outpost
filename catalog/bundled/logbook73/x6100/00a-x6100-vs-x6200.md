# X6100 or X6200? What changed

The X6100 and X6200 plainly belong to the same family: both put an HF and 6 m software-defined radio, colour spectrum display, waterfall, automatic antenna tuner and portable power system into one carryable station. That resemblance makes it tempting to treat the X6200 as an X6100 revision and reuse one set of instructions for both. Do not. The newer radio has a different control layout, a different battery arrangement, substantially expanded settings and applications, and its own firmware history.

This chapter is the bridge between the two companion guides. It helps an X6100 owner understand what really changes, and it helps a prospective owner decide which workflow suits them. It is not a buying ranking: a lighter menu system can be an advantage in the field, while more built-in tools can be worth the extra learning at a desk.

## The short answer

Choose the **X6100 guide** when the label on the radio says X6100. Its current stock baseline is **APP V1.2.0 with BASE V1.1.9**. It has the familiar six left-edge button menus, stock computer-assisted FT8 through `USB-DIG`, and an internal battery.

Choose the **[X6200 guide](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/overview)** when the label says X6200. Its current guide baseline is **firmware V1.0.7**, including earlier V1.0.6 additions that were not removed or relocated. It has its own SETTING, DISPLAY, SYSTEM and application pages; a detachable battery system; expanded spectrum, DSP and wireless features; and firmware-version-specific tools such as the FT8 decoder and CW decoder.

Never follow an update package, factory-reset sequence, menu path or battery procedure merely because it worked on the other model. Confirm the exact model and the versions shown by that radio first.

## What genuinely carries across

The radio theory carries across almost completely. On either set you still need to:

- connect a suitable antenna or dummy load before transmitting;
- stay within your licence, band plan and the transmit limits enabled for your market;
- choose LSB or USB for voice by band convention, CW for Morse and the correct data path for computer sound-card modes;
- run the lowest power that completes the contact;
- interpret SWR as an antenna-system measurement rather than a score for the tuner;
- use RF gain, preamp/attenuator, filters and AGC deliberately instead of turning every processing control up;
- record settings and firmware versions before a reset or update.

The operating rhythm also feels related. You tune against a live spectrum, watch signals build on the waterfall, select a mode and filter, match the antenna with the built-in tuner, then make or log the contact. If you already know one radio, the other will feel recognisable within minutes.

What does **not** carry across is the exact gesture or path. A familiar concept may sit on a different page, use a different label or have extra choices. Learn the idea once; then use the exact-model quick reference to find it.

## Where the X6100 remains simpler

The X6100's interface is mature and relatively compact. Its front panel and left-edge menus divide the radio into memorable groups: `GEN` for broad settings, `KEY` for CW, `DFN` and `DFL` for noise and filters, `APP` for utilities, and `MSG` for stored messages. The guide follows that physical structure.

APP V1.2.0 changed a few details that older videos and cheat sheets may not show. In particular:

- `AGC SLOW` and `AGC FAST` are release-time values in milliseconds, replacing older KNEE/SLOPE wording;
- the release guidance favours slow behaviour for AM/SSB voice and fast behaviour for CW;
- `CENTER MODE` off enables the movable spectrum cursor;
- `HANDLE F1` and `HANDLE F2` assign hand-microphone shortcuts;
- `SYSTEM INFO` is where you verify the installed APP and BASE versions;
- the official update requires the prescribed factory reset afterwards.

For stock FT8, keep the distinction especially clear: the computer does the encoding, decoding and contact timing, while the X6100 supplies CAT and audio. The radio must be in **`USB-DIG`** (shown as `U-DIG` on some displays), not ordinary USB voice. Community SD-card software can add a different on-radio experience, but that is a separate environment and should not be mistaken for stock firmware.

The X6100 therefore remains attractive when you want a well-understood portable station and are happy for a laptop or tablet to handle advanced digital work. Fewer on-radio application branches also means fewer version-dependent paths to remember.

## What the X6200 expands

The X6200 gives the newer platform more room. Its settings include extra microphone, line-audio and equalisation controls; separate display groups for spectrum, waterfall, audio scope and colours; system networking; and a broader application set. Firmware releases have continued to change the detail, so the [X6200 Menu Reference](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/menu-reference) identifies the baseline and the [X6200 Firmware chapter](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/connectivity-firmware) labels later additions.

Examples of the expanded surface include:

- five-band receive, wide-FM and microphone equalisation groups;
- RF FFT, audio FFT, waterfall and audio-scope presentation controls;
- display power, backlight and screen-saver settings;
- expanded, model-specific WLAN, Bluetooth and WFSERVER controls (the X6100 also has WFSERVER);
- modem, SWR scanner, FT8 decoder and voice-call applications;
- a KEY-page CW decoder added in V1.0.6;
- network information, FT8CN Bluetooth and WR12-related support added in V1.0.6;
- V1.0.7 AGC/NR changes, extra SSB tuning steps and AM filters, waterfall speed choices, `SHOW RX LEVEL`, expanded receive coverage and a 3D bandscope.

That list illustrates why “X6100 plus a few options” is the wrong mental model. Some features did not exist on the earlier radio, some moved between X6200 firmware releases, and some require an accessory or external app. The dedicated guide separates what was in the English manual from what a later release added, moved or removed.

More controls do not automatically mean a better signal. Equalisation cannot repair overload, NR cannot rescue a signal outside the passband, and a 3D display does not improve propagation. The newer tools are useful when you know what question each answers; otherwise the safe installed defaults are a sound starting point.

## Battery and update habits are not interchangeable

This is the area where casual model substitution is least acceptable. The X6100's internal battery and the X6200's detachable system lead to different physical preparation. Follow the power limits, charging instructions and update tutorial written for the exact model.

Before updating either radio:

1. Photograph or write down settings that matter.
2. Read the release notes for the exact package you intend to install.
3. Check the model name, installed version and destination version on the radio itself.
4. Use a stable external supply and do not interrupt the process.
5. Follow the model-specific sequence, including any battery-removal or multi-stage instruction.
6. Perform a factory reset only when the official instructions for that release require it, then restore settings deliberately rather than importing assumptions from another version.

On the X6100, record **both APP and BASE** in `SYSTEM INFO`; two different numbers are normal because the components update separately. On the X6200, follow its own system/baseband process. A firmware file being published by the same manufacturer does not make it safe for both radios.

## Which guide should an existing owner read?

If you own an X6100, stay in this guide for procedures. The X6200 chapters are useful only as a product comparison and should not be used as instructions for your radio.

If you own an X6200, start with [Why the X6200 is a separate radio](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/overview), then work through its getting-started and menu-reference chapters. Return here only when comparing the family or translating a concept you already learned on the X6100.

If you are choosing between them, decide from workflow rather than a feature count:

- choose the X6100 approach if you value the established compact interface, internal battery and a computer-based stock digital workflow;
- choose the X6200 approach if you value the detachable battery system, broader display/DSP configuration and newer on-radio/network applications enough to learn their firmware boundaries;
- choose neither solely because a third-party video shows one impressive screen — first confirm whether it is stock firmware, community software or an accessory-dependent feature.

## Try it yourself

If both radios are available at a club or shop, compare them without transmitting:

1. **Identify before touching.** Read the model label and open the version screen. Record X6100 APP/BASE separately; record the X6200's displayed versions using its own SYSTEM page.
2. **Find the same concept.** Locate TX power, AGC, filter width, spectrum reference and WLAN on each. Notice that the purpose carries across but the path does not.
3. **Compare the displays usefully.** Tune the same busy band and adjust only the spectrum reference or span. Ask which display helps you find and centre a signal, not which looks more dramatic.
4. **Trace an FT8 workflow.** On the X6100, find `USB-DIG` and the computer audio/CAT path. On the X6200, find the FT8 decoder documentation and identify exactly what it decodes versus what still needs an external operator or application.
5. **Return every changed setting.** Restore the starting values, or factory-reset only if the owner and the correct manual explicitly call for it.

## Key takeaways

- The X6100 and X6200 share radio concepts, not interchangeable controls, batteries or firmware.
- The X6100 guide is currently based on stock APP V1.2.0 and BASE V1.1.9; verify both in `SYSTEM INFO` on your unit.
- Stock X6100 FT8 uses a computer and **`USB-DIG` / `U-DIG`**, not ordinary USB voice.
- The X6200 has separate, expanded settings and firmware-added applications, so it has its own [complete companion guide](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/overview).
- Release-dependent features must be labelled by version, especially decoders, networking, display additions and update steps.
- When in doubt, trust the model label, the version screen and the official procedure for that exact radio—not family resemblance.

For X6100 operation, continue with [Getting Started](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6100/getting-started). For the newer set, go to [Getting Started with the X6200](outpost:entry/logbook73.guide.x6200/getting-started).
