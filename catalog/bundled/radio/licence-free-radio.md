# Radio you can use without an amateur licence

Two-way radio services that members of the public can use in the United Kingdom, Europe and the United States without passing an amateur radio exam: PMR446 and Citizens' Band (CB) in the UK and Europe, and FRS, GMRS, MURS and CB in the United States. Channel numbers, frequencies and power limits are from the regulators' own documents.

## The law in brief

"Licence-exempt" does not mean unregulated. Each service may be used without an individual licence only with equipment that meets its rules: the right frequencies, power and antenna. Using the wrong radio, or the right radio outside its rules, is transmitting without a licence. One United States service here, GMRS, does need a licence. The rules are national: a radio that is legal in one country can be illegal in the next. This sheet is a summary for reference, not legal advice.

In particular, American FRS, GMRS and MURS radios must not be used in the UK. Ofcom says that FRS equipment "cannot be used within the UK on a licence-exempt basis" and that its use "is likely to cause harmful interference to frequencies used by the Home Office". Many imported hand-held radios can transmit on all of these channels at powers no licence-exempt service allows; being able to tune a channel is not permission to use it.

## PMR446 (UK and Europe)

PMR446 is the walkie-talkie service on 446 MHz. In the UK the rules are:

| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency band | 446.0 to 446.2 MHz |
| Maximum power | 500 mW effective radiated power (ERP) |
| Equipment | Hand-portable, with an integral (fixed) antenna only |
| Not allowed | Base stations, repeaters, use as part of a network, and use from aircraft |
| Protection | None: users must choose a channel that does not interfere with others |

The European decision gives the lowest carrier frequency and the spacing (446.00625 MHz and 12.5 kHz; 446.003125 MHz and 6.25 kHz). The channel numbers above are the usual numbering in order of frequency, worked out from those figures.

Abroad: analogue PMR446 is harmonised across Europe, so a compliant analogue radio bought in the UK may be used in other European countries that apply the decision. Digital PMR446 has been part of the same European decision for the whole band since 1 January 2018, but Ofcom's information sheet (2018) warns that it was not then harmonised everywhere. Check the country before using digital channels, or channels 9 to 16, abroad.

## CB radio in the UK

CB is licence-exempt in the UK on two sets of forty channels: the "CEPT" or "EU" band used across Europe, and a UK-only band. Channels are 10 kHz apart and each is used for both transmitting and receiving.

| Mode | Band | Maximum power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FM | CEPT (EU) band and UK band | 4 W transmitter carrier power (4 W ERP as well for a radio with an integral antenna) |
| AM (double sideband) | CEPT (EU) band only | 4 W ERP |
| SSB (single sideband) | CEPT (EU) band only | 12 W ERP, measured as peak envelope power |

Ofcom notes that CB "is not afforded any protection" from interference.

## Other licence-exempt short range devices in the UK

Ofcom's interface requirement IR 2030 lists many kinds of licence-exempt short range device. The one most often found in hand-held radios is the "non-specific short range device" allocation at 433 MHz, sometimes sold as LPD433:

| Frequencies | Maximum power | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 433.05 to 434.79 MHz | 10 mW ERP | Duty cycle of 10 per cent or less |
| 433.05 to 434.79 MHz | 1 mW ERP | |
| 434.04 to 434.79 MHz | 10 mW ERP | Duty cycle up to 100 per cent if the bandwidth is 25 kHz or less |

At these powers the range is very short. These frequencies are inside the 70 cm amateur band, and Ofcom notes that some of them line up with the input frequencies of amateur repeaters.

## FRS and GMRS (United States)

The Family Radio Service (FRS) and the General Mobile Radio Service (GMRS) share the same twenty-two channels. What differs is the licence and the power.

| | FRS | GMRS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licence | None: authorised by rule | An individual FCC licence is required |
| Who may hold one | Not applicable | A person aged 18 or over |
| Who may operate | Any person | The licence holder and their immediate family; anyone, if needed to pass an emergency message |
| Licence term | Not applicable | Normally ten years |

## MURS (United States)

The Multi-Use Radio Service has five VHF channels and needs no individual licence. The rules name the channels by frequency; the numbers 1 to 5 often printed on radios are simply this order and are not in the rules.

| Order | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 151.820 MHz |
| 2 | 151.880 MHz |
| 3 | 151.940 MHz |
| 4 | 154.570 MHz |
| 5 | 154.600 MHz |

Transmitter power output must not exceed 2 W. The antenna may be no more than 18.3 metres (60 feet) above the ground, or 6.1 metres (20 feet) above the structure it is mounted on.

## CB radio in the United States

The Citizens Band Radio Service needs no individual licence. Its forty channels are the frequencies in the "CEPT (EU) band" column of the table above.

| Mode | Maximum power |
| --- | --- |
| AM or FM voice | 4 W mean carrier power |
| SSB voice | 12 W peak envelope power |

Channel 9 (27.065 MHz) "may be used only for emergency communications or traveler assistance". On every channel, operators "must, at all times and on all channels, give priority to emergency communications".

## Not covered

- Rules in individual European countries, including which of them allow CB on AM and SSB and at what power, and which allow digital PMR446.
- A revision of ECC Decision (15)05 was under way in 2026; the version read here is the one amended in March 2018.
- Canada, Australia and other countries with their own services (for example UHF CB in Australia).
- Business radio licences, and the short-range and "simple" licences Ofcom sells for a fee.
- Tone squelch settings (CTCSS and DCS), which do not make a channel private.
- Equipment standards and certification. A radio must be approved for the service it is used in, whatever frequencies it can be programmed with.
