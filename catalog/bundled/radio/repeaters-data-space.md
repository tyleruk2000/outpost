# Repeaters, data modes and space: band-plan frequencies

Where amateurs put repeaters, APRS, FT8 and other data modes, and the frequencies of the amateur station on the International Space Station. Everything here comes from the band plans of the International Amateur Radio Union (IARU) or from ARISS, the body that runs the space station's amateur radio.

## The law in brief

All of these frequencies are inside amateur bands, so transmitting on them needs an amateur licence that covers the band in the country you are in. A band plan is a voluntary agreement among amateurs, not a rule from the regulator: it tells you what to expect on a frequency, not what you are allowed to do. Listening to amateur transmissions is lawful in the UK and the United States. This sheet is a reference, not legal advice.

National band plans add detail and sometimes differ from the regional plan. In the UK that is the plan published by the Radio Society of Great Britain, and in the United States the plan published by the ARRL together with local repeater co-ordination. Neither is reproduced here. For a particular repeater, use a current repeater listing: this sheet gives the regional pattern only, and does not give access tones.

## 2 metres in Region 1 (UK, Europe, Africa, Middle East)

From the IARU Region 1 VHF band plan. The band is 144 to 146 MHz.

| Frequency | Use in the band plan |
| --- | --- |
| 144.050 MHz | Telegraphy (CW) calling |
| 144.300 MHz | SSB centre of activity |
| 144.500 MHz | Image modes centre (SSTV, fax) |
| 144.600 MHz | Data centre of activity |
| 144.800 MHz | APRS |
| 144.8125, 144.8250, 144.8375, 144.8500 and 144.8625 MHz | Digital voice internet gateways |
| 144.975 to 145.194 MHz | Repeater inputs (the frequency you transmit on), exclusive |
| 145.2375, 145.2875 and 145.3375 MHz | FM internet voice gateways |
| 145.375 MHz | Digital voice calling |
| 145.500 MHz | FM calling |
| 145.575 to 145.7935 MHz | Repeater outputs (the frequency you listen on), exclusive |
| 145.194 to 145.206 MHz and 145.794 to 145.806 MHz | Space communication |
| 145.806 to 146.000 MHz | Satellites only |

The repeater outputs are 600 kHz above the inputs: to use a 2 metre repeater in Region 1 you listen on its output and transmit 600 kHz lower. The plan allows FM and digital voice a bandwidth of 12 kHz in these segments.

## 2 metres in Region 2 (the Americas)

From the IARU Region 2 band plan. The band is 144 to 148 MHz.

| Frequency | Use in the band plan |
| --- | --- |
| 144.200 MHz | CW and SSB weak-signal calling |
| 144.300 MHz | CW and SSB calling |
| 144.390 MHz | APRS centre of activity |
| 144.600 to 144.900 MHz | Repeater inputs; outputs 600 kHz higher |
| 145.200 to 145.500 MHz | Repeater outputs; inputs 600 kHz lower |
| 145.790 to 145.800 MHz | Guard band: no transmission |
| 145.800 to 146.000 MHz | Satellites only |
| 146.010 to 146.370 MHz | Repeater inputs; outputs 600 kHz higher |
| 146.520 MHz | FM calling |
| 146.600 to 146.990 MHz | Repeater outputs (last channel 146.970 MHz); inputs 600 kHz lower |
| 146.990 to 147.400 MHz | Repeater inputs (first channel 147.000 MHz); outputs 600 kHz higher |
| 147.590 to 148.000 MHz | Repeater outputs; inputs 600 kHz lower |

So the regional pattern in the Americas is also a 600 kHz shift, but which side is the input depends on the part of the band. This is the regional plan as written. National plans and local co-ordination can differ from it, including for the pairs above 147 MHz, so check a local repeater listing before relying on a shift.

Other Region 2 entries worth knowing: 435 to 438 MHz is for satellites only, and in the Caribbean 145.010 MHz is to be protected for APRS.

## The International Space Station

From ARISS (Amateur Radio on the International Space Station). The station's crew and its automatic equipment use these frequencies; what is active changes, so check ARISS for the current status.

| Use | Frequency | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice downlink | 145.800 MHz | Worldwide |
| Voice uplink | 144.490 MHz | ITU Regions 2 and 3 (the Americas, the Pacific and southern Asia) |
| Voice uplink | 145.200 MHz | ITU Region 1 (Europe, Russia and Africa) |
| VHF packet, uplink and downlink | 145.825 MHz | Worldwide |
| UHF packet, uplink and downlink | 437.825 MHz | |
| Repeater uplink | 145.990 MHz | With a 67 Hz access tone |
| Repeater downlink | 437.800 MHz | |
| SSTV downlink | 437.550 MHz | As listed by ARISS when checked |

Anyone may listen. ARISS notes that the higher-power radio on the station can be received nearly from horizon to horizon with a simple hand-held radio or scanner. Transmitting to the station needs an amateur licence.

## Not covered

- Repeater access tones (CTCSS), individual repeaters, and the national repeater plans of the UK and the United States. None of the documents read gives them.
- Repeaters and calling frequencies on 70 cm and other bands, and the 6 metre and 4 metre repeater channels.
- JS8, PSK, RTTY and other data modes: the plans read give no single frequency for them.
- Amateur satellites other than the space station. See AMSAT for those.
- HF emergency nets and their schedules. Only the emergency centres of activity agreed by the IARU are given, in the emergency frequencies sheet; nets run by RAYNET, ARES and similar groups were not checked with those groups and are left out.
- Winlink, packet bulletin boards, DMR, D-STAR and other networked systems.
