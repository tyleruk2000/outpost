# Amateur radio bands in the United Kingdom

Every frequency band in the Ofcom amateur radio licence, with the power limit for each of the three licence levels: Foundation, Intermediate and Full. The figures are those in Schedule 1 of Ofcom's Amateur Radio Licence Conditions Booklet (OFW611), as linked from Ofcom's website on 15 November 2026.

## The law in brief

Transmitting on these bands needs an Ofcom amateur radio licence of the right level, or the direct supervision of a licence holder. This sheet is a summary for reference, not legal advice: the licence itself is what counts, and Ofcom can change it. Other countries have different bands and limits.

Listening is a separate matter. Ofcom says receivers and scanners can typically only be used for "general reception", such as licensed broadcasting stations and hobby radio, which includes amateur radio.

## Licence levels and power

Power is peak envelope power (PEP) at the transmitter unless the table says ERP or EIRP. ERP is power radiated relative to a half-wave dipole; EIRP is relative to an isotropic antenna.

| Licence level | Usual limit | Where it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | 25 W (13.98 dBW) | Most bands up to 440 MHz; 2 W or 1 W in the microwave bands |
| Intermediate | 100 W (20 dBW) | Most bands |
| Full | 1000 W (30 dBW) | Bands where the amateur service is primary |
| Full | 400 W (26 dBW) | Most bands where the amateur service is secondary |

"Full" in the tables covers the Full licence, the Full (Club) licence and the Full (Temporary Reciprocal) licence. A dash means the band is not available at that level.

Use from an aircraft is allowed only where the Airborne column says so, and then at no more than 500 mW EIRP. Above 10 W EIRP (6.1 W ERP) the licence also requires an assessment of electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure.

## The 5 MHz band (Full licence only)

The 5 MHz band is not one continuous band. It is these eleven segments, all secondary and available only where they do not interfere with other services inside the UK or Crown Dependencies.

| 5 | 5313 kHz | 5323 kHz |
| 6 | 5333 kHz | 5338 kHz |
| 7 | 5354 kHz | 5358 kHz |
| 8 | 5362 kHz | 5374.5 kHz |
| 9 | 5378 kHz | 5382 kHz |
| 10 | 5395 kHz | 5401.5 kHz |
| 11 | 5403.5 kHz | 5406.5 kHz |

Extra conditions apply on 5 MHz (Note G of the Schedule): 100 W PEP and no more than 200 W EIRP; a double-sideband signal no wider than 6 kHz; an antenna no higher than 20 metres above the ground; no interference to Ministry of Defence use, and close down if you learn you are causing it; operation only at the main station address or a temporary location in the UK or Crown Dependencies; and you must be reachable on a telephone close to the radio. Contact with military and cadet stations is allowed in this band only.

## What the licence says about emergencies

Condition 7(5) of the licence says the licensee "may use or permit the use of the Radio Equipment to assist with communications: a) in times of disaster or local, national, international emergency; b) to support operations conducted by a user service; or c) during any exercise relating to a and b." The licence defines a user service as the British Red Cross, St John Ambulance, the St Andrew's Ambulance Association, the Royal Voluntary Service, the Salvation Army, any Government Department, and any Category 1 or Category 2 responder under the Civil Contingencies Act 2004. Ofcom's guidance describes this as allowing licensees "to provide emergency communications to support the response to an incident or training for one".

## Not covered

- Mode-by-mode band plans. The Ofcom licence does not divide bands by mode; the Radio Society of Great Britain publishes voluntary UK band plans, which are not reproduced here.
- Calling frequencies above 146 MHz (for example on 70 cm): the IARU document read for this sheet stops at 146 MHz.
- Repeaters, beacons, gateways and data stations, which have their own power limits and may need a Notice of Variation.
- Call signs, regional secondary locators, operating abroad, and the electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure rules.
- Special research permits and anything Ofcom allows by individual variation.
