# Amateur radio bands in the United States

The frequency bands the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) gives to each class of amateur operator licence, with the mode sub-bands and power limits the rules set. The figures are from Part 97 of the FCC rules (47 CFR Part 97) as published in the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, current to 13 October 2026.

## The law in brief

Transmitting on these bands needs an FCC amateur operator licence of the right class, or a foreign licence the FCC rules recognise. This sheet is a summary for reference, not legal advice: the rules themselves are what count, and they change. Other countries have different bands and limits.

Listening is not restricted for these services: the Communications Act (47 U.S.C. 605) says its ban on intercepting and passing on radio messages "shall not apply" to a communication "transmitted by an amateur radio station operator or by a citizens band radio operator", to stations transmitting for the general public, or to one that "relates to ships, aircraft, vehicles, or persons in distress".

## Licence classes

The rules name five classes: Novice, Technician, General, Advanced and Amateur Extra. This sheet gives a column to each of Amateur Extra, Advanced, General and Technician. A Novice has the same HF segments as a Technician, and above 30 MHz only 222 to 225 MHz and 1270 to 1295 MHz.

The tables give the bands for ITU Region 2, which is the Americas and so nearly all of the United States. A station in a US territory in Region 3 (the western Pacific) or in Region 1 has slightly different edges on some bands; see section 97.301 of the rules.

## The 60 metre band

The 60 m rules changed in 2026. Amateurs may transmit in the band 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz and on four separate channels. No emission may be wider than 2.8 kHz. Amateurs must not cause interference to, and must accept it from, the other services that share these frequencies.

| Part of the band | Centre frequency | Carrier frequency for sideband phone and data | Maximum power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Channel | 5332.0 kHz | 5330.5 kHz | 100 W ERP |
| Channel | 5348.0 kHz | 5346.5 kHz | 100 W ERP |
| Band | 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz | - | 9.15 W ERP |
| Channel | 5373.0 kHz | 5371.5 kHz | 100 W ERP |
| Channel | 5405.0 kHz | 5403.5 kHz | 100 W ERP |

ERP here is transmitter PEP multiplied by antenna gain relative to a half-wave dipole. On the four channels a CW signal is sent on the centre frequency.

## Mode sub-bands below 30 MHz

A CW (Morse) signal may be sent on any frequency the operator is allowed to use. The rules then divide most HF bands between RTTY and data at the bottom and phone and image above.

An operator can only use the part of a sub-band that falls inside the segments for their licence class. For example, a General may use phone on 20 m from 14.225 to 14.350 MHz.

Phone and image are also allowed from 7.075 to 7.100 MHz, but only for stations in ITU Regions 1 and 3, and for stations in Region 2 that are west of 130 degrees West or south of 20 degrees North.

## What the rules say about emergencies

Section 97.403: "No provision of these rules prevents the use by an amateur station of any means of radiocommunication at its disposal to provide essential communication needs in connection with the immediate safety of human life and immediate protection of property when normal communication systems are not available."

Section 97.405(a): "No provision of these rules prevents the use by an amateur station in distress of any means at its disposal to attract attention, make known its condition and location, and obtain assistance." Paragraph (b) says the same for a station helping one in distress.

Section 97.401: a station in Alaska, or within 92.6 km of it, may use 5.1675 MHz (single sideband, 150 W PEP at most) for emergency communications, sharing the channel with the Alaska private fixed service.

These sections are about amateur stations. They do not give an unlicensed person a general permission to transmit.

## Not covered

- The sharing rules for each band (section 97.303) beyond the few noted, and the full list of emission standards (section 97.307).
- Stations in US territories outside ITU Region 2, repeaters, beacons, auxiliary and space stations, and automatic control.
- Band plans by mode and activity, which are voluntary and published by amateur radio societies. ARRL's band plan is not reproduced here.
- State and local restrictions, and FCC radio-frequency exposure rules.
