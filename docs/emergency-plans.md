# Emergency plans

**Preparedness → Emergency plans** holds your household's own plans for a power cut,
flooding, no phone or internet, severe weather (storm, snow and ice, heatwave), sheltering
at home, leaving at short notice, or anything else. A plan is written on a quiet evening
to be read on a bad one, perhaps on a phone by torchlight, perhaps by someone who did not
write it: short, ordered, plain, printable.

Every plan carries one line, on screen and on every card: *This is our household's own
plan, made in advance. In a real emergency, follow the emergency services and official
warnings. Emergency services: 999.* The number is the plan's own.

Plans are kept on the server: in backups, counted under Storage → Database, and erased
by Erase everything. A plan is saved a moment after each change. If another device saved
it since, nothing is overwritten: choose **Keep my version** or **Load the other
version**. Ticking steps never causes this.

## What a plan holds

Every part may be empty.

- **What it is for**: a kind and your own words; the **number for the emergency
  services** where you are; how often to ask for a review.
- **Steps** for before, during and after, in order.
- **The household**: each person, what they need to remember, anything medical for their
  wallet card, and their number from Contacts. The people of the plan's household are
  offered by name (**Add Maya**); anyone else can be typed in. Pets are not offered.
- **Who to contact, in order**, one marked as the out-of-area contact.
- **Where to meet** if the home or the neighbourhood cannot be used.
- **If the phones fail**: what you will do, a radio channel and when to listen, and a
  communication plan under Radio.
- **Supplies**, for the household or each person, for the whole time or each day.
- **Power**: a power plan, with how long its batteries run its loads.
- **Where things are**: the stopcock, the fuse board, the gas valve, the torch.
- **Notes and links** to notes (for photos), library documents and saved places.

Contacts, places, plans and documents are linked, not copied. If one is deleted, the plan
keeps its name and says it is missing ("Ana Silva: no longer in Contacts"); nothing is
dropped silently.

**Review.** A plan shows when it was last opened and last looked over, and after the
period you chose (3 months, 6 months, a year, or never) asks to be looked over.

**Households.** Each household has its own plans. The list shows the plans of the
household Outpost is set to; with more than one household, "For Caravan · Change" under
the title says which. A plan opened from a link that belongs to another household says so.
A copy of a plan is made in the current household. A plan kept on a phone carries its
household's name, and says it out of reach too.

## Templates

A new plan starts from a template or blank. Every step of a template comes from official
guidance, and says whose words it is:

- **From** a named source (publisher, page, the page's own date), with its words, address
  and licence a press away;
- **Changed from the words of** a source, once you edit it, with the original beside it;
- **A pointer, not advice**: Outpost's words naming a page to read, for a publisher whose
  words may not be copied; put your own words in its place;
- **Your own words**.

Steps you take out can be put back. Outpost writes no safety advice of its own: where no
usable official source covers something, the template asks you under **For you to
decide**. The radiation emergency template is in
[nuclear-preparedness.md](nuclear-preparedness.md#the-radiation-emergency-template).

### Templates from other governments

Where Home is in a country Outpost has templates from (else where your device is set to),
those come first in the list, and the dialog says whose they are:

- **Australia**: *Leaving home* and *Storm or cyclone*, from the Queensland Government's
  disaster pages, CC BY 4.0. Queensland is one state: check your own state's emergency
  service as well.
- **The Netherlands**: *The first 72 hours at home*, from the government's Denk vooruit
  pages (in English) and Rijksoverheid.nl, CC0. A few lines are put into English from the
  Dutch; the Dutch words are shown beside them. The kit list, with its water figure, is an
  infographic whose words may not be copied: read it on Denk vooruit.

Elsewhere the dialog says the templates are mostly UK guidance, and to check every step
against your own government's advice. Germany's BBK allows no copying without permission;
the New Zealand, Irish, Canadian and Australian federal guidance could not be read.

### Whose words are used

Only the words of publishers whose terms allow copying are shipped. Their terms, quoted,
are in [sources.md](sources.md#emergency-plan-templates-what-is-quoted-and-on-what-terms-read-2026-11-16).

- **Quoted**: GOV.UK and the Environment Agency, Be Ready (Cabinet Office) and the Met
  Office, under the Open Government Licence v3.0; Ofcom, unchanged, by its permission.
  Their licence statements are on the screens and printouts.
- **Pointed to, not quoted**: the NHS (Be Ready's heat advice, which points to it, is used
  instead), the British Red Cross, Ready.gov and FEMA. New Zealand Police's number is
  given as a fact.

Quotes were checked word for word against the pages as read on 16 November 2026. Water
follows Be Ready: "3 litres per person per day" for at least 3 days.

**Left to you**, as no usable official source covers them: how to reach each other with
no phone (a time and place, a radio channel, a note at home); a word that proves a message
is from one of you; a contact outside the area, a second meeting place and which rest
centre (ask your council); generators, and medicines that must be kept cold; which room to
shelter in and when to come out; what to do after a cold spell or heatwave; who holds a
spare key and who checks on neighbours.

### The number for the emergency services

Each plan keeps its own, offered from the household's country (its own choice, else the
country its home is in; with no home, your device's language) and changeable. Outpost
offers a number only where it has the country's own published source: 999 or 112 in the
UK, 112 across the European Union and in Switzerland and South Africa, 911 in the United
States and Canada, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand. Anywhere else it offers none and
says so ("Not known for Brazil: add your local number"); the plan, Use now and the cards
say the number is not given until you type it.

## Supplies, beside the inventory

The plan's supplies are set against **Supplies**:

- **Days of food and water held**, from Supplies, against the days the plan wants.
- **Each thing listed** against the inventory by name ("Torch (battery or wind-up)"
  matches "LED torch"), in the same unit, leaving out anything past its use-by: **in
  stock**, **low**, **missing**, or **check** (held in another unit: compare by hand).
- **One press** puts every missing and low thing on the Supplies shopping list.

## Using a plan

**Use now** shows the plan in large type for the moment it is needed: the emergency
number, then **What to do now** (the During steps), who to call, where to meet, where
things are, what to do if the phones fail, each person's own line and the notes, then the
After steps. Links under the number jump to each part. The Before steps come last, folded,
with how many are done. A number calls on a phone; a place opens the offline map. **Start using this plan** records when it
started; each tick is kept with its time, so whoever opens it next sees what is done.
**Reset for next time** clears the ticks. A tick on one phone never undoes an edit on
another. Once started, it links to **Check in on the household board**
([messages.md](messages.md)).

On a phone, add the page to the Home Screen from the browser's Share menu. **Keep on this
phone**, at its foot, lets it open with the server out of reach (on a secure page); ticks
are sent when it is back ([offline.md](offline.md#an-emergency-plan)).

### Warnings held for the home place

The list of plans and Use now show one line when Outpost holds a warning for the home
place ([weather.md](weather.md#places)):

> “Yellow warning of wind affecting London & South East England” was in force for your
> area when Outpost last checked, 3 hours ago: open Weather and hazards

The words are the publisher's; with several, the most severe is named. Met Office
warnings fetched over 24 hours ago, flood warnings over 12 hours ago, and warnings whose
period has ended are not shown. It never says what to do.

## Cards to print

**Cards to print** makes PDFs on the device:

- **Wallet cards**, one per person: a strip that folds to bank-card size (85.6 × 54 mm),
  four to a sheet, with cut and fold marks. Front: name, own number, medical notes, the
  emergency number. Inside: who to call (up to three), the out-of-area contact, where to
  meet. If a card cannot fit, the screen says whose and what to shorten.
- **The fridge sheet**: the steps for during and after, the numbers, meeting places and
  how to communicate, on one page as large as fits.
- **The grab list**: the plan's supplies with amounts and a box to tick.

Each offers **Save as PDF**, **Add to library** (which asks first: the cards hold names,
numbers and where you will be) and **Share**. Print at actual size. With more than one
household, each card, sheet and list names the household it is for ("For Caravan"), and so
does the file's name.
