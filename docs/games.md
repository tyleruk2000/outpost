# Games

Small games for when the internet is out, under **Games**. None of them reaches the
internet; there is no sound.

## What is kept, and where

- **The allotment and the Steading** are kept on the server, one for the household.
  **Start again** at the foot of each clears it for everyone, after asking.
- **Each game's tally** (played, won, best time, fewest moves) is on the server, shared
  by the household; **Clear the tally** under the board forgets it.
- **An unfinished game**, Snake's scores and Skerry's progress stay in the browser they
  are played in (not kept in a private window), so two devices never overwrite each
  other.

Games are in every backup and removed by erase everything.

## Allotment

A small plot of beds, and a small almanac of real growing in Britain. Sow, water,
harvest, sell at the stall for coins, and buy seeds, beds and a few lasting things.

**Nothing is ever lost.** A crop out of its months, or in dry soil, waits with all its
growth; a ripe crop waits for ever. Radish seed and onion sets are free, so the plot is
never stuck. There are no streaks, rewards, notifications or ending.

### The rules

A crop grows only while it is **in its months** (from its earliest sowing to its last
harvest in Britain; under glass, every month) **and its bed is moist**: for 12 hours
after watering with the can, and in the open all through a wet day and 12 hours after it
(2 days with the **water butt**). Under glass only the can waters. Whether a day is wet
comes from the date, the same on every device: 30% of days from April to July, rising
to 50% in November and December.

The season is the real month. The hemisphere is northern unless Home is south of the
equator; the switch on the screen overrides it. In the south, the months move on six.

### The crops

The months come from the Royal Horticultural Society's "grow your own" pages
([sources.md](sources.md#the-allotments-months-and-facts-read-2026-11-17)). Growing time
(in moist soil, in its months), prices and yields are made up.

| Crop | Grows (in the open) | Growing time | Seed | Yield | Sells each |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Radishes | March to October | 4 minutes | free | 3 | 1 |
| Salad leaves | February to December | 25 minutes | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Onions | all year | 1 hour | free | 3 | 2 |
| Peas | February to October | 2 hours | 10 | 5 | 4 |
| Carrots | February to November | 6 hours | 15 | 6 | 5 |
| Beetroot | March to October | 12 hours | 20 | 5 | 8 |
| Kale | all year | 18 hours | 20 | 6 | 7 |
| Broad beans | October to August | 1 day | 25 | 6 | 9 |
| Potatoes | March to October | 1 day | 30 | 8 | 7 |
| Leeks | all year | 2 days | 40 | 5 | 18 |
| Garlic | October to August | 2½ days | 35 | 6 | 16 |
| Squash | April to October | 3 days | 60 | 3 | 45 |

You start with 10 coins and 4 beds. The next bed costs 25 × (beds − 3) coins, up to 12
beds.

Lasting things:

- **Water butt**, 40: the open beds stay watered 2 days after each wet day.
- **Compost bin**, 80: each harvest adds 1 compost (at most 8); a sowing uses one, and
  that bed yields 2 more.
- **Hens**, 120: an egg every 3 hours, at most 6 waiting, 3 coins each.
- **Beehive**, 200: honey every 8 hours, at most 4 waiting, 12 coins each.
- **Apple tree**, 300: an apple every 6 hours, at most 12 waiting, 3 coins each.
- **Greenhouse**, 500: two more beds under glass, where every crop grows all year.

A harvest the basket cannot hold (9,999 of each kind) stays in its bed.

### Reading the plot

A bed's label shows the time left, **Ripe**, or why it waits ("Dry", "Till Mar"); tap it
for what would change it. **This month on the plot** says what to sow now; sowing out of
season is never refused, but the list says first what will happen. **The crops** has a
card per crop: its months, how it takes cold and dry, and, once grown, a few facts.

It is not a planting guide for any one garden: the southern months are Britain's moved
on six, and how long water lasts and how often it rains are made up.

## Steading

A settlement for the household to build, on a land you choose. Everything is made from
something and carried from somewhere: a bakery with no flour bakes nothing.

**Leave it as long as you like: nothing here ever gets worse while you are away.** Work
goes on until the stores and yards are full, then stops. Nothing decays or leaves; there
are no streaks, timers, reminders or events. Only batteries and diesel run down, and a
flat battery only slows the machines until the sun or wind is back.

### Choosing the land

A new settlement begins by choosing the land and the seed. The same land and seed draw
the same 48 by 48 map on any Outpost, so a seed can be read out to share a map; it is
shown under the game's heading. **Another seed** draws a new one.

Each land is drawn from a real place, and its sun, wind and cold are that place's:

| Land | Drawn from | 4.8 kW array a day, June / December | 10 kW turbine a day | Firewood | Grain | Also |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| River valley | Keswick, Cumbria | 17 / 2.6 kWh a day | 13 kWh | 100% | 100% | no ore: it comes by barge |
| Mountains | Davos, Switzerland (1,551 m) | 20 / 2.4 | 0 | 167% | 31% | rock, coal and ore close by; houses need 8 more bricks |
| Snow and tundra | Kiruna, Sweden | 19 / 0 | 2 | 167% | 52% | lakes, few trees, ore; houses need 10 more bricks |
| Desert | Ouarzazate, Morocco | 24 / 24 | 6 | 55% | 150% | wells draw 400 litres, not 1,000; fields within 2 squares of water; trees only by the river |
| Coast | Belmullet, Ireland | 19 / 3.6 | 68 | 78% | 137% | the sea along the west; little rock |
| Forest | Freudenstadt, Black Forest | 20 / 6.6 | 3 | 98% | 142% | woods everywhere; houses need 4 more bricks |

Firewood and grain are against the river valley: firewood follows the cold and a field's
harvest the warmth. The wells, extra bricks and where fields may stand are made up.
Every map has woods, rock and clay near the start, so a new one is never stuck. A settlement founded before lands could be chosen keeps its small valley,
Ashgill.

### The map

Drag to move the map, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the buttons. With the keyboard,
Tab to the plan, move with the arrow keys, + and − to zoom, and Enter to choose a
square. Every square has a spoken name ("Column 24, row 18, Woodcutter, working"), and
every building is also in the list beside the plan.

### Production chains

- Choose a square of open ground to build on it. Each building shows what it uses and
  makes in a day, its workers and its cost. Pits, mines, quarries and woodcutters stand
  beside what they take; the water wheel and waterworks beside the river. Woodcutters,
  quarries, clay pits and fields are free.
- A building sends what it makes to a workshop that uses it or to a storehouse, along
  **belts** (below) or **routes**: choose the building, then **Send its … to…**, the place
  and how it is carried. By hand carries 12 a day up to 3 squares, free; a cart road 40
  up to 8 squares, for a plank and a stone a square; track (once 40 people live here)
  160 up to 20 squares, for a bar of iron and a plank a square.
- A building fills its first route first, then its belts (**Fill this first** changes
  the order). What cannot be sent waits in its yard (20 of each thing); a full yard rests
  the work. **Carry the yard to the store** empties it by hand.
- The chains: logs to a sawmill (planks) or a woodyard (firewood); clay and firewood to a
  brick kiln; grain to a mill, flour and firewood to a bakery; ore and coal to a
  smelter, iron and planks to a smithy.
- Each building says what holds it back (workers, an input, a full yard), and a flag on
  the plan marks it. The **ledger** lists every good: in store (200 of each per
  storehouse), kept each day, used by households, and made in all.
- Taking a building down gives back everything it and its routes cost.

### Belts

Choose a square of open ground and **Lay a belt from here**, then choose squares one
after another; a square further along the same row or column fills in the run. A belt
takes from the building its first square leads away from, and carries into the building
its last square leads into, if that building uses what is sent (or is a storehouse).

- A **roller belt** carries 60 a day (a plank a square), a **chain belt** 240 (a plank
  and a bar of iron, once 24 people live here). Belts that meet merge; a **junction**
  (twice the cost) lets two cross. A belt into nothing, off the land, or in a loop
  carries nothing.
- A belt square's panel turns it, upgrades it or takes it up. A settlement keeps at
  most 600 squares of belt.

### Households

A new settlement has a storehouse, two households of four and a well. People work within
4 squares of home. A new house fills once it has a well within 3 squares, a storehouse
within 5, and enough water, bread and firewood for everyone; otherwise it says what it
waits for. Households never leave.

A household of four uses in a day, from real figures
([sources.md](sources.md#the-steadings-household-figures-read-2026-11-21)): 9,000 kcal
of food (about 4.2 loaves of 800 g), 60 litres of water (the top of WHO's range), 6.8 kWh
of electricity, and 26 kWh of heat, about 6.3 kg of dry logs in the river valley (a
typical household's gas counted as wood, ignoring the difference in efficiency). On
other lands two thirds of the heat follows the land's heating degree days; the third for
hot water and cooking stays the same (the game's rule).

### Power

Once 24 people live here, power can be built. Houses use it for light; a sawmill, mill
or smithy runs twice as fast on 40 kWh a day.

| Building | What it is | Makes or holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water wheel | beside the river | 120 kWh a day (made up for the game) |
| Solar array | twelve 400 W panels, 4.8 kW peak | the land's sun for the month, in daylight |
| Wind turbine | 10 kW, 7 m rotor; may stand on a fell | the land's wind for the month |
| Generator | 10 kW on diesel, 200-litre tank | only when the batteries are at their floor |
| Lead-acid battery | 24 cells of 2 V 1,000 Ah | 48 kWh; 50% used, 80% round trip |
| Lithium battery | 16 LiFePO4 cells of 3.2 V 280 Ah | 14.3 kWh; 80% used, 92% round trip |

The sun, the wind and the wheel come first, charging the batteries with any surplus; a
shortfall comes from the batteries down to their floor, and only then from a generator
(about 73 litres of diesel a day at full load). When everything falls short, the
machines go without first, then the lights. Current limits are not counted, and an array
makes its energy evenly through the daylight (a real one makes most at noon). The
**Power** section shows what is made and wanted, the batteries and tanks (**Fill the
tank from the store**). Panels, battery cells and diesel come by barge.

### Trade

Once 12 people live here a **landing** on the river or the shore can be built. Choose a
trade for it (planks, bricks or bread for ore; grain for coal; bricks for diesel) and lay
routes or belts to and from it: the barge takes and brings that every day. The landing
also buys anything for credit, and sells coal, ore, iron, tools, diesel, panels and
cells, at prices that never change.

### Time

A day in the settlement passes in an hour, and every rate is per such day; the month is
the calendar's. The server works the settlement out exactly, however long it was left.
A clock that jumps back changes nothing.

Real: each land's sun, wind and cold, the turbine, the batteries, the generator's fuel
use and the household figures
([sources.md](sources.md#the-steadings-lands-and-power-read-2026-11-21)). Made up: every
rate, cost, reach and price, the belts, the water wheel, and how heat and harvest follow
the land.

It has not been tried on a real phone.

## Solitaire

Klondike, draw one or draw three. A tapped card goes to its best place (the setting under
the board switches to pick and put), and cards can be dragged. **Put away all that can
go** sends every card it can to the foundations. Undo takes back every move; time counts
only while the tab is visible.

## Peg solitaire

The English board of 33 holes, all filled but the centre. Jump a peg along a row or
column over a neighbour into an empty hole; the jumped peg is removed. Tap a peg, then a
marked hole, or drag it. One peg left is solved, best of all in the centre.

## Sudoku

Each puzzle is made on the device and has exactly one answer. **Gentle** (38 givens) and
**Steady** (30) need only singles; **Testing** has as few givens as one answer allows.
Pencil notes, undo, erase, and **Check**, which marks wrong cells without showing an
answer. The keyboard works too.

## Draughts

English draughts, the rules shown under the board: captures are compulsory, kings move
one square either way, and 40 moves each with no capture and no man moved is a draw.
Against the computer, play dark or light. Undo takes back a whole turn.

## Chess

Chess by the FIDE Laws, against the computer or two at one device, the rules under the
board. Every rule is played, including castling, en passant and promotion to any piece.
Threefold repetition and the 50 moves end the game at once rather than waiting for a
claim; dead positions other than insufficient material are not spotted.

Tap a piece to see its legal squares, then tap one or drag. **Copy the game** gives PGN
and FEN; **Paste a game** reads either. The computer is a small search of Outpost's own,
with no opening book or endgame tables. **Learner** looks 1 ply ahead and blunders often (a child who knows the moves can beat
it); **Easy**, 2 plies, takes what is left hanging; **Medium**, 3, is a steady opponent
for a casual player; **Hard**, 4, is a fair game for one, but any club player or chess
program will beat it.

None has been rated against people. On a Raspberry Pi or an old phone it is slower.

## Pairs

Turn over two cards at a time; a match stays up, a miss turns back. Small is 6 pairs,
medium 8, large 12, with pictures a small child can name. No clock is shown.

## Snake

Steer a snake to the apples on a 16 by 16 field; each apple adds a cell and a point. It
ends when the head hits the body, or the edge unless **Wrap round** is on. Filling the
field wins. Steer with the arrow keys, W A S D, a swipe or the buttons under the field,
at four speeds. Space or P pauses; it pauses itself when the page is hidden.

## Skerry

You are the relief keeper of the weather station on Tarrow Skerry, a made-up island. The
last keeper, Ines, left her notes in the station computer, which is worked by typing
commands. Each of the twelve levels asks one question, and the files hold the answer.
Play any level, in any order.

- `help` lists commands, `man <command>` explains one, `goal` repeats the question, and
  `answer` gives yours (`answer 5`). Some levels are solved by reading the right file.
- `hint` gives as many hints as you like; the last all but says what to type.
- The commands behave like the real ones (`ls`, `cd`, `cat`, `grep`, `sort`, `find` and
  others, joined with `|`); `decode 3 note.txt` undoes a Caesar shift of three.
- Tab completes names and the arrow keys bring back what you typed; on a phone, buttons
  under the terminal do the same.

The command line is pretend: it reads only the level's made-up files, changes nothing,
runs nothing on the device or on Outpost, and reaches no network.
