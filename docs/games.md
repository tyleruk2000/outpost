# Games

Small games for when the internet is out, under **Games** in the navigation, in the
phone's More sheet and in the quick search. None of them reaches the internet; there is
no sound.

Allotment, Steading, Solitaire, Peg solitaire, Sudoku, Draughts, Chess, Pairs, Snake and
Skerry.

## What is kept, and where

- **The allotment and the Steading** are kept on the server, one for the household.
  **Start again** at the foot of each clears it for everyone, after asking.
- **Each game's tally** (played, won, best time, fewest moves) is on the server, shared
  by the household. **Clear the tally** under the board forgets it, after asking.
- **An unfinished game**, Snake's scores and Skerry's progress stay in the browser they
  are played in, so two people on two devices never overwrite each other. In a private
  window a game plays the same and is not kept.

Games are in every backup and removed by erase everything.

## Allotment

A small plot of beds. Sow seeds, water them, harvest, take the produce to the stall for
coins, and buy seeds, more beds and a few lasting things. It is also a small almanac of
real growing in Britain.

**Nothing is ever lost.** Nothing withers or dies: a crop out of its months, or in dry
soil, waits with all its growth; a ripe crop waits for ever. Radish seed and onion sets
are free, so the plot is never stuck. Eggs, honey and apples stop at a small cap while
you are away. There are no streaks, rewards, notifications or ending.

### The rules

A crop grows only while it is **in its months** (from its earliest sowing to its last
harvest in Britain; under glass, every month) **and its bed is moist**:

- for 12 hours after watering with the can (watering again tops it up);
- in the open, all through a wet day and 12 hours after it;
- in the open with the **water butt**, all through a wet day and 2 days after it.

No rain falls under glass and the butt does not reach the greenhouse: there, only the
can waters. Whether a day is wet comes from the date, so it is the same on every device:
30% of days from April to July, rising to 50% in November and December (northern months).

The season is the real month. The hemisphere is northern unless Home (the saved place
with the home icon) is south of the equator; the switch on the screen overrides it. In
the south, the months, the crops' months and the rain move on six.

### The crops

The months a crop grows in come from the Royal Horticultural Society's "grow your own"
pages ([sources.md](sources.md#the-allotments-months-and-facts-read-2026-11-17)); each
crop's card in the game gives its sowing and harvest months. Growing time (in moist soil,
in its months), prices and yields are made up.

| Crop | Grows (in the open) | Growing time | Seed | Yield | Sells each |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Radishes | March to October | 4 minutes | free | 3 | 1 |
| Salad leaves | February to December | 25 minutes | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Onions | all year | 1 hour | free | 3 | 2 |
| Peas | February to October | 2 hours | 10 | 5 | 4 |
| Carrots | February to November | 6 hours | 15 | 6 | 5 |
| Beetroot | March to October | 12 hours | 20 | 5 | 8 |
| Kale | all year | 18 hours | 20 | 6 | 7 |
| Broad beans | October to August | 1 day | 25 | 6 | 9 |
| Potatoes | March to October | 1 day | 30 | 8 | 7 |
| Leeks | all year | 2 days | 40 | 5 | 18 |
| Garlic | October to August | 2½ days | 35 | 6 | 16 |
| Squash | April to October | 3 days | 60 | 3 | 45 |

You start with 10 coins and 4 beds. The next bed costs 25 × (beds − 3) coins, up to 12
beds.

| Lasting thing | Costs | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water butt | 40 | Keeps the open beds watered for 2 days after each wet day. |
| Compost bin | 80 | Each harvest adds 1 compost (at most 8); a sowing uses one, and that bed yields 2 more. |
| Hens | 120 | An egg every 3 hours, at most 6 waiting; 3 coins each. |
| Beehive | 200 | A jar of honey every 8 hours, at most 4 waiting; 12 coins each. |
| Apple tree | 300 | An apple every 6 hours, at most 12 waiting; 3 coins each. |
| Greenhouse | 500 | Two more beds under glass: every crop grows all year, but no rain falls. |

Coins stop at 999,999 and each kind in the basket at 9,999; a harvest the basket cannot
hold stays in its bed.

### Reading the plot

- A bed's label shows the time left, **Ripe**, or why it waits ("Dry", "Till Mar"). Tap a
  bed for what would change it.
- **This month on the plot** says what to sow now and what is waiting for what. Sowing
  out of season is never refused; the list says first what will happen.
- **The crops** has a card per crop: its months, how it takes cold and dry, and, once
  grown, its family and a few facts.

How long water lasts and how often it rains are made up too. It is not a planting guide
for any one garden: the southern months are Britain's moved on six.

## Steading

A settlement in Ashgill, a made-up valley of woods, rock, clay banks and a river.
Everything is made from something and carried from somewhere: a bakery with no flour
bakes nothing.

**Leave it as long as you like: nothing ever gets worse while you are away.** Work goes
on while it is closed until the stores and yards are full, then stops. Nothing decays,
runs out or leaves; there are no timers, reminders or events. A day in the settlement
passes in an hour.

### Production chains

- Tap a square of meadow to build. Each building shows what it uses and makes in a day,
  its workers and its cost. Pits and quarries go beside what they dig; the landing,
  water wheel and waterworks beside the river. Woodcutters, quarries, clay pits and
  fields are free.
- Choose a building, then **Send its … to…**, a workshop or a storehouse, and how it is
  carried:

  | Carried | Per day | Reach | Costs a square |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | By hand | 12 | 3 squares | nothing |
  | Cart road | 40 | 8 squares | a plank and a stone |
  | Track (40 people) | 160 | 20 squares | a bar of iron and a plank |

- What cannot be sent waits in the building's yard (20 of each thing); a full yard stops
  the work. **Fill this first** orders the routes; **Carry the yard to the store**
  empties a yard by hand. A flag marks a building that is held back, and it says why.
- The **ledger** lists every good; a storehouse holds 200 of each.
- Taking a building down gives back everything it and its routes cost.

### Households

You start with a storehouse, two households of four and a well. People work within 4
squares of home. A new house fills once it has a well within 3 squares, a storehouse
within 5, and enough water, bread and firewood coming in for everyone. Households never
leave.

A household of four uses, in a day, from real figures: 9,000 kcal of food (about 4.2
loaves; 2,250 kcal a person, Public Health England; 266 kcal per 100 g of bread, USDA),
60 litres of water (15 a person, the top of WHO's range for basic needs), 26 kWh of heat
(about 6.3 kg of dry logs: a typical household's gas, Ofgem, at 4.1 kWh/kg, Forest
Research; stove and boiler efficiency are not distinguished) and 6.8 kWh of electricity
(Ofgem). Electricity is not needed to move in. A **water wheel** (24 people) makes 120 kWh a day: it lights houses within 5 squares
first, then runs the sawmill, mill and smithy within reach at twice the pace. The
**waterworks** (40 people) waters every house in the valley.

### Trade

The valley has no ore. Once 12 people live there, build a **landing** on the river,
choose a trade (planks, bricks or bread for ore; grain for coal) and lay routes to and
from it; the barge takes and brings that every day. The landing also buys anything from
the store for credit and sells coal, ore, iron and tools. Prices never change.

Every rate, cost, reach and price is made up; the household figures are real.

## Solitaire

Klondike, draw one or draw three. A tapped card goes to its best place (its foundation,
else the next pile it fits on, else an empty pile); the setting under the board switches
to pick and put, and cards can be dragged. **Put away all that can go** sends every card
it can to the foundations and offers to finish when that is possible. Undo takes back
every move; time counts only while the tab is visible.

## Peg solitaire

The English board of 33 holes, all filled but the centre (d4). Jump a peg along a row or
column over a neighbour into an empty hole; the jumped peg is removed. Tap a peg, then a
marked hole, or drag it. One peg left is solved, best of all in the centre. The tally
keeps the fewest pegs left in a game played out.

## Sudoku

Each puzzle is made on the device and has exactly one answer. **Gentle** (38 givens) and
**Steady** (30) need only singles; **Testing** has as few givens as one answer allows and
needs more. Pencil notes, undo, erase, and **Check**, which marks wrong cells without
showing an answer. The number pad shows how many of each digit are left; the keyboard
works too. On a narrow phone the cells are smaller than Outpost's usual 44 px so that
nine fit across; the pad's keys are full size.

## Draughts

English draughts, the rules shown under the board: captures are compulsory, kings move
one square either way (not flying kings), and 40 moves each with no capture and no man
moved is a draw. Against the computer, play dark or light; it looks 3 moves each ahead.
Undo takes back a whole turn.

## Chess

Chess by the FIDE Laws (in force from 1 January 2023), against the computer or two at
one device, the rules under the board. Every rule is played, including castling, en
passant and promotion to any piece. Threefold repetition and the 50 moves end the game
at once rather than waiting for a claim; dead positions other than insufficient material
(such as locked pawn chains) are not spotted.

Tap a piece to see its legal squares, then tap one or drag. **Turn the board** shows it
from the other side. **Copy the game** gives PGN and FEN; **Paste a game** reads either.

The computer is a small search of Outpost's own, with no opening book, endgame tables or
chess engine:

| Level | Looks ahead | Plays like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner | 1 ply | Blunders often: a child who knows the moves can beat it. |
| Easy | 2 plies | Takes what is left hanging, misses most plans. |
| Medium | 3 plies | A steady opponent for a casual player. |
| Hard | 4 plies | A fair game for a casual player; any club player or chess program will beat it. |

None has been rated against people. On a Raspberry Pi or an old phone it replies more
slowly.

## Pairs

Turn over two cards at a time; a match stays up, a miss turns back. Small is 6 pairs,
medium 8, large 12. The pictures are things a small child can name. No clock is shown
while playing.

## Snake

Steer a snake to the apples on a 16 by 16 field; each apple adds a cell and a point. It
ends when the head hits the body, or the edge unless **Wrap round** is on. Filling the
field wins.

Steer with the arrow keys, W A S D, a swipe or the buttons under the field. Four speeds,
from slow (for small children) to fast. Space or P pauses; it pauses itself when the page
is hidden.

## Skerry

You are the relief keeper of the weather station on Tarrow Skerry, a made-up island. The
last keeper, Ines, left her notes in the station computer, which is worked by typing
commands. Each of the twelve levels asks one question, and the files hold the answer.

- Type a command and press Enter. `help` lists commands, `man <command>` explains one,
  `goal` repeats the question, and `answer` gives yours (`answer 5`). Some levels are
  solved by reading the right file.
- `hint` gives a hint, as many as you like; the last one all but says what to type.
- The commands behave like the real ones (`ls`, `cd`, `cat`, `grep`, `sort`, `find` and
  others, joined with `|`); `decode 3 note.txt` undoes a Caesar shift of three.
- Tab completes names; the arrow keys bring back what you typed. On a phone, the buttons
  under the terminal do the same and type `|`. Play any level, in any order.

The command line is pretend: it reads only the level's made-up files, changes nothing,
runs nothing on the device or on Outpost, and reaches no network.
