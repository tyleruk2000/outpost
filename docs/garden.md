# Tools: Garden

**Tools → Garden** says what to sow, plant and harvest each month in the UK, from the Royal
Horticultural Society's calendar, and keeps a record of what you sowed. The calendar works
with no internet.

## The month

It opens on this month; **Month** shows another.

- **Sow**: what the RHS sows this month. Tender crops say how frost affects them.
- **Plant or plant out**: crops planted (potatoes, garlic, onion sets, leeks, tomatoes) or
  raised indoors and planted out.
- **Harvest**: what is picked or lifted.

It covers 22 vegetables, with the RHS's months for the UK as a whole. A late spring, high
ground or a sheltered garden moves them by weeks. If home is outside the UK the screen warns
that the months may be wrong there.

## Your last frost

Give the day your last spring frost usually comes and press **Save last frost**; **Clear it**
goes back to the RHS months.

- A tender crop (courgettes, cucumbers, French and runner beans, squash, sweetcorn,
  tomatoes) is not listed for planting out before the day after it; in that month it says
  "from" the day.
- If your date is after every month the RHS gives for planting a crop out, the screen says to
  keep it under cover.
- An early date does not bring planting forward, and sowing, harvest and hardy crops are not
  moved.

Outpost does not guess a last-frost date: no published rule turns monthly temperatures into
one. The Met Office says the last air frost usually comes before the end of April in the
south and near the coast, and into May or June further north and higher up. Ground frost can
come when the air stays just above freezing. Your own records, or a neighbour's, are best.

## What you sowed

**Record a sowing**: the crop (or **Something else**), the day sown, and if you like where,
how much, when planted out, the first harvest and notes. Until a harvest is recorded, a
crop's line says when the RHS harvests it. **Change** edits; **Remove** asks first. The
record and last-frost date are in every backup and erased with everything else.

It gives months, not days; vegetables only, UK only; and no frost warnings (see
**Preparedness → Weather and hazards**).

Sources: the RHS "Grow your own" page for each crop (listed on the screen) and the Met
Office's "Why frost can still occur in late spring", read on 21 November 2026
([sources.md](sources.md#the-garden-calendar-read-2026-11-21)).
