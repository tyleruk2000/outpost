# Tools: Garden

**Tools → Garden** says what to sow, plant and harvest in a month in the UK, from the Royal
Horticultural Society's calendar, and keeps a record of what the household sowed. The
calendar works with no internet; the record and the last-frost date are kept on this
Outpost.

## The month

The screen opens on the current month. **Month** shows another.

- **Sow**: the crops the RHS sows in that month. Tender crops carry a line on how frost
  affects them (most are sown indoors before the last frost and outside only after it).
- **Plant or plant out**: crops planted (potatoes, garlic, onion sets, leeks, tomatoes) or
  raised indoors and planted out.
- **Harvest**: the crops picked or lifted in that month.

The calendar covers 22 vegetables. The months are the RHS's for the UK as a whole. A cold
or late spring, high ground, or a warm sheltered garden moves them by weeks.

If home (the saved place with the home icon on the map) is outside the UK, the screen says
so: Outpost has no published calendar for other countries yet, and the UK months may be
wrong there.

## Your last frost

Give the day your last spring frost usually comes, as a month and a day, and press **Save
last frost**. **Clear it** goes back to the RHS months as they are.

With a date set:

- A tender crop (courgettes, cucumbers, French and runner beans, squash, sweetcorn,
  tomatoes) is not listed for planting out before the day after it. In the month of the
  frost it says "from" that day. Earlier months list it as kept back.
- If the date is after every month the RHS gives for planting a crop out, the screen says to
  keep it under cover or grow it in a greenhouse.
- A date earlier than the RHS months does not bring planting forward. The RHS gives no rule
  for that.
- Sowing and harvest months, and every hardy crop, are not moved.

Outpost does not work out a last-frost date from the weather screen's climate averages:
they hold monthly mean temperatures, and no published rule turns those into a date. The Met
Office says the last air frost usually comes before the end of April in the south and near
the coast, and well into May, or even June, further north and on higher ground. Ground
frost, which damages tender plants, can come on nights when the air stays just above
freezing. Your own records, or a neighbour's, are the best guide.

## What you sowed

**Record a sowing** asks for the crop (or "Something else" and its name), the day it was
sown or planted, and, if you like, where, how much, the day it was planted out, the day of
the first harvest, and notes. Each line in the list shows when it was sown and where. Until
a first harvest is recorded, a calendar crop's line says when the RHS harvests it.

**Change** edits a sowing. **Remove** takes it out of the record after asking. Nothing is
removed any other way.

The record and the last-frost date are part of every backup, are counted under Manage and
clean up, and are erased with everything else.

## What it does not do

- It gives months, not days, except for the day after your last frost.
- It does not watch the weather or warn of a frost tonight: see Preparedness → Weather and
  hazards.
- It covers vegetables only, and only the UK.
