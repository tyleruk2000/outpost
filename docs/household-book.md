# The household book

**Preparedness → Household book** asks a few questions about your household and makes a
printable book from the answers and from what Outpost already holds: your emergency plans
and a fridge sheet for each, contacts, first aid, water, frequencies, journeys, checklists,
grab cards and more. Print all of it, or only the sections you choose (only the fridge
sheets, say, or only the grab cards). It is made on the
device you press **Make the book** on, and saved, added to the library or shared like
every PDF Outpost makes.

## The questions

Six steps. Your answers are saved each time you move on (**Save and go on**), so you can
stop (**Save and stop here**) and come back on any device. **Change the answers** takes
you through them again.

1. **People and pets.** Everyone at home, from **Supplies → Household**. A person added
   here is added there, since that is where the food and water they need is worked out.
   For each person you can say what they need ("uses a wheelchair") and carry ("inhaler
   in coat pocket"), and tick **A card of their own** for a child's card. Medical sheets
   are made under **Medical sheets**.
2. **Home.** The saved place with the home icon. With none, mark it on Maps, or type its
   latitude and longitude and press **Save as home**. The emergency number is filled in
   from the country home is in where Outpost knows it; type another to use that.
3. **Where you would go and meet.** Meeting points near home and outside the area, and
   the people you would go to. Each is a saved place on the map; a person can also be a
   contact, whose saved place then stands in for theirs.
4. **Vehicles and radios.** Each vehicle's range on a full tank or charge, and the kinds
   of radio set you have.
5. **How long, and which hazards.** The days to be ready for, the hours you would walk in
   a day on a long journey, and the hazards that matter to you. A hazard you have an
   emergency plan for starts ticked, and says which plan. A ticked hazard with no plan
   offers **Make a plan for this**: it opens **Emergency plans** with that template
   chosen, and **Back to the household book** brings you back to this step. Under **Your
   emergency plans**, choose whether all of them go in the book or only some.
6. **What goes in the book.** The household's name for the cover, the paper (A4 or US
   Letter, first set from the country home is in), the sections, blank pages for notes,
   and whether each medical sheet goes in in full. Untick sections to print only some.

## What is in the book

A cover with the number to call, what changed since the last book and what the book
cannot know; the contents; then each section you chose, numbered, starting on a page of
its own; then the sources. Every page has the book's name and its page number at the
foot.

| Section | Made from |
| --- | --- |
| The first few minutes | Official guidance on going in or getting out, your meeting points, check-in time and first contact |
| Fridge sheets | One page for each emergency plan in the book: the number, what to do, who to call, where to meet |
| Our household | Supplies' household, your answers, and the medical sheets that can be read on this device |
| Keeping in touch | Contacts marked for emergencies, and the communication plan, with a page for each person |
| Home and meeting points | A map of home and every meeting point from the offline map on this device, each place in several grid formats, and the people you would go to |
| First aid | Lines of the NHS website's pages, unchanged, and FEMA's words on the recovery position, with drawings of them |
| Help near home | Hospitals, pharmacies, emergency services, shelters, drinking water, food banks and shops in the reference layers, with distance and direction |
| Places to keep away from | Nuclear and military sites, airports and fuel stations in the reference layers, and the wind at home |
| What to do, by situation | Every emergency plan in the book, in full and as you wrote it; for a hazard with no plan, the template's general advice, marked as such |
| Water | How much for your people and days, how to make it safe (quoted, with drawings of a layered filter, catching rain and boiling), and drinking water near home |
| Food, toilet and hygiene | The energy your household needs, food after a power cut or a flood, keeping clean with little water |
| Warmth, light and the radio | Official guidance on keeping warm and lit |
| Radio frequencies | The communication plan's channels first, then licence-free channels, emergency broadcasting, distress and calling, broadcasters your sets can hear and repeaters near home, each with whether it needs a licence |
| Journeys | Each place you would go to, on foot and by car, with a drawing of the route |
| Checklists | A shopping list for your people and days, the grab bag, the first aid kit and the car, with a box for **Got it** and **To get** |
| Supplies | What is kept, where and until when, and how long it lasts |
| Local risks | Flood and emergency planning zones at home where a layer is held, and the climate's extremes |
| Grab cards | A card for each person, to cut out: medical lines, who to call, where to meet, home's position |
| Cards for the children | A card each, to cut out |
| Pages for notes | Ruled pages |

Each emergency plan's steps are your own and are printed as your plan. Steps that came
from a template keep their source, listed at the back. **Emergency plans** and each plan
say whether it is in the book, with **Put in the household book** when the book takes
only some, and **Make the book again** when the book is older than the plans.

Something the book needs and Outpost does not hold is said where it belongs, with how to
get it: a reference layer (**Maps → Layers**), the climate for home (**Weather and
hazards → Climate**), terrain for the journeys, a communication plan.

## Journeys

Outpost has no road routing, so each journey is the straight line from home, and the
book says so. The time on foot is worked out along that line with Tobler's hiking
function (5 km/h on level ground, slower up and down slopes), from the terrain installed:
for a fit adult with no load, and at half that pace for someone hurt, tired or with small
children. Where each day's walking ends follows from the hours a day you gave, and is
named after the nearest town in the reference layers. Without terrain the line is taken as
level. By car the book compares each vehicle's range with the straight line, and asks you
to keep a paper road map.

## The wind at home

The book reads the average direction the wind comes from at home from the climate
averages kept under **Weather and hazards** (NASA POWER). A site within 45° of that
direction is marked upwind. Averages fetched before Outpost asked for the wind's direction
have none: fetch them again.

## Where the words come from

Every piece of advice is quoted from a named publisher and listed at the back with the
date it was read. Official guidance is the emergency plan templates' (mostly the UK
government's Be Ready pages, under the Open Government Licence); a book for a home outside
the UK says so. First aid is whole lines of the NHS website's pages, unchanged and
credited as its terms ask. The recovery position is FEMA's words, which are free of
copyright. The NHS no longer has pages of its own on adult CPR, choking or severe
bleeding, and St John Ambulance, the British Red Cross and Resuscitation Council UK do not
allow theirs to be reprinted, so the book points to them instead. Clearing cloudy water
and catching rain are the CDC's and the US Army's survival manual's words. It holds no
guidance on toilets without water.

Each drawing shows what the words beside it say and no more. Where the words leave
something open (the US Army's filter names its materials but no order, and no bottle), the
caption says so.

## What changed since

Outpost keeps a few lines about each book it makes. The next book's cover lists what was
added or taken out in each section since. Make the book again whenever the household, its
plans or its places change, and replace the printed copy.

When anything the last book was made from has changed or been deleted since (the answers,
emergency plans, contacts, saved places, medical sheets, Keeping in touch, supplies and the
household), Home and the book's screen say which, with a link to make it again. Facts read
round home (help nearby, the wind, terrain) are not watched. A household binder made
before the binder became part of the book stands in for the book until the book is made.

## What it does not do

- It does not know whether a place listed is open, stocked or still there.
- It does not follow roads or paths.
- It does not give medical advice of its own, and checks no dose.
- It does not list power stations other than nuclear ones, chemical works or major hazard
  sites: Outpost holds no openly licensed copy of them.
- An encrypted medical sheet is in the book only once opened on this device, and is then
  in the clear on paper. Adding the book to the library asks first.
