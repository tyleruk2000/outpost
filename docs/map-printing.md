# Printing a map

**Print or save as PDF**, on the Maps screen, makes a one-page paper map with a title, date,
scale bars, north, latitude and longitude round the edge, and the credits. It is made on your
device from the installed maps; nothing is sent anywhere until you choose **Add to library**.

## Making a page

1. Press **Print or save as PDF**. A box appears: **the box is the page**. Drag it, or its
   handles; **Centre the box here** moves it to the middle of the map.
2. Choose the paper (A4, A3 or US Letter), which way up, and the scale: **Fit the box**, or
   1:10,000 to 1:250,000. On a phone these are in the sheet; lower it to move the box.
3. **Preview the page**. Give it a title, choose a **Map style** (**Low ink** saves ink), and
   tick what to show: names, contour lines, hill shading, road numbers, routes, saved places,
   **Magnetic north**, latitude and longitude lines, UTM grid lines, Maidenhead squares, the
   grey line, an estimated radio range, and what was heard on the radio. The preview warns when the installed maps or terrain do not cover the page in
   enough detail (1:25,000 needs zoom 13). An estimated radio range is printed in the light
   map's green whatever the screen shows (the dark theme, the night style).
4. **Make the PDF**, then **Save as PDF**, **Add to library**, **Share** or **Print**.

**Magnetic north** adds an arrow, the declination for the middle of the sheet that day with
whether to add or subtract it, and blue lines every 30 mm to set a compass against.

At 1:25,000 an A4 portrait page covers about 4.7 × 6.3 km and A3 landscape 10 × 6.3 km.

## Scale, and how far to trust it

- **Print at 100% ("actual size"), not "fit to page".** The page says how long its scale bar
  should be ("the 1 km bar is 40 mm long"): measure it once.
- The stated scale is exact along the middle of the sheet only; the page prints the scale at
  its top and bottom edges. On a walking map it is good to about 1%; on a sheet spanning 10°
  of latitude it is out by up to 13% away from the middle.
- True north is straight up everywhere on the sheet.
- Opposite edge ticks are the ends of one line of latitude or longitude, so a ruler between
  them gives a position. British National Grid lines are not drawn.

## Quality and the file

- Drawn at 300 dots to the inch (**Draft** is 150). The map is a picture; the title, scale,
  figures and credits are real text, and hidden text lists what the map shows, so a search
  finds the page.
- A phone draws a large page in pieces; if it still cannot, the resolution is lowered and the
  dialog tells you.
- A title in a script other than western European letters goes in as a small picture.
- **The credits** are printed with their web addresses. Do not crop them off.

## Saving, sharing, and adding the page to the library

- **Save as PDF** keeps the file on the device only.
- **Add to library** files it under "Made in Outpost" as "Map, *title*, 1:25,000, *date*",
  searchable and in backups ([library.md](library.md)). On a phone this does not depend on the
  phone saving a file.
- **Share**, where the device can share files (HTTPS needed).

The same page made twice on one day is the same file.

## The browser's own Print

Ctrl or ⌘ P prints the sheet alone, but browsers add margins and do not all honour the page
size, and a phone's browser may not print at all. The PDF is the dependable way
([encryption.md](encryption.md#printing-from-a-phone)).

## Limits

One page, no atlas of sheets. The area cannot cross the 180° meridian. A paper map is fixed
on the day it is made, and only as good as the installed map and terrain
([Contour lines](maps.md#contour-lines)).
