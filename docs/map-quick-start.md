# Map quick start

For anyone in the household who did not set Outpost up. Everything here is on the **Maps**
screen and works with no internet once a map is installed.

## Before you start: is there a map?

If the map shows only a plain outline of the world, none is installed.

1. Open the **Offline** tab beside the map (on a phone, drag the sheet at the bottom up).
2. Pick a ready-made region, or draw a box round the area you want.
3. Tick **Include terrain for hill shading and contour lines**: heights, line of sight and
   radio range need it too.
4. Press **Check the size**, then **Download**.

This needs the internet once.

## Find a place

1. Type in the **search box** at the top of the map: a place name (once place names are
   downloaded, see [Offline place search](maps.md#offline-place-search)), a postcode or grid
   reference such as `NY 215 072` in Great Britain, or coordinates such as
   `54.4609, -3.0886`.
2. Choose a result. The map moves to it.
3. To keep it, press **Save this place** and give it a name. It is then under **Places**.

Or press and hold the map (right-click with a mouse) and choose **Save a place here**.

## Read the height and the grid reference

The line at the foot of the map gives the position of the cross in the middle.

1. Move the map until the cross is on the spot. (With a mouse the line follows the pointer
   and says "Pointer".)
2. Press the arrow at the end of the line and choose **British National Grid** for a grid
   reference, or degrees, UTM, MGRS or a Maidenhead locator.
3. Read the height after the coordinates: `NY 21540 07210 · 812 m`. On a phone, open the
   line out to see it.
4. Press the coordinates to copy them.

No height shows where no terrain is installed. It is the ground thereabouts, not a surveyed
spot height. Metres or feet: **Settings → Appearance**.

## Print a map

1. Press **Print or save as PDF**. A box appears: the box is the page.
2. Drag it over the area. Choose the paper, which way up, and the scale (1:25,000 suits
   walking).
3. Press **Preview the page**, give it a title, and tick what to show.
4. Press **Make the PDF**, then **Print** or **Save as PDF**.

Print at 100% (not "fit to page") so the scale is right. More in
[Printing a map](map-printing.md).

## Check line of sight between two points

For a radio on VHF or above: can two places see each other over the ground?

1. Press **Line of sight between two points**.
2. Tap the map at each end. A saved place puts the end exactly on it.
3. Give each aerial's height above the ground (1.5 m for a radio in the hand) and the band.
4. Read the answer: **Clear**, **In sight, with the Fresnel zone partly blocked**,
   **Blocked**, or **Not known** where terrain is missing.

If they cannot see each other, **Find high ground in sight of both** suggests relay places.
This is geometry over terrain, not a promise that a signal gets through.

## See how far a radio could reach from a point

1. Press and hold the map at the spot (right-click with a mouse).
2. Choose **Estimated radio range from here**.
3. Ground in sight of an aerial there is shaded green. The dashed magenta circle is the
   typical range (half the directions reach further, half not as far); grey rings mark
   10, 25, 50 and 100 km, only as far as the ground in sight reaches.

The card under the map gives the answer in a few words; press **Show the details** (the
upward arrow) for the rest. Set the heights under **Heights and details**. Woods, buildings and power are not counted:
radio often reaches less than this.
