# Map quick start

For anyone in the household who did not set Outpost up. Each part is a few steps on the
**Maps** screen. Everything here works with no internet once a map is installed.

## Before you start: is there a map?

If the map shows only a plain outline of the world, no map is installed yet.

1. Open the **Offline** tab beside the map (on a phone, drag the sheet at the bottom up).
2. Pick a ready-made region, or draw a box round the area you want.
3. Tick **Include terrain for hill shading and contour lines**: heights, line of sight
   and radio range need terrain too.
4. Press **Check the size**, then **Download**.

This needs the internet once. After that the map works without it.

## Find a place

1. Type in the **search box** at the top of the map:
   - a place name, once place names are downloaded (see [Offline place search](maps.md#offline-place-search));
   - in Great Britain, a postcode or a grid reference such as `NY 215 072`;
   - or coordinates, such as `54.4609, -3.0886`.
2. Choose a result. The map moves to it.
3. To keep it, press **Save this place** on its card and give it a name. It is then
   under the **Places** tab.

You can also press and hold the map (right-click with a mouse) and choose **Save a place
here**.

## Read the height and the grid reference

The line at the foot of the map gives the position of the cross in the middle of the map.

1. Move the map until the cross is on the spot. (With a mouse, the line follows the
   pointer instead, and says "Pointer".)
2. Press the arrow at the end of the line and choose how to show it: **British National
   Grid** for a grid reference, or degrees, UTM, MGRS or a Maidenhead locator.
3. Read the height after the coordinates, for example `NY 21540 07210 · 812 m`. On a
   phone the height shows once the line is opened out.
4. Press the coordinates themselves to copy them, to send or paste into a note.

No height is shown where no terrain is installed. The height is that of the ground
thereabouts, not a surveyed spot height. Metres or feet is under **Settings → Appearance**.

## Print a map

1. Press **Print or save as PDF** in the column of buttons on the map. A box appears:
   the box is the page.
2. Drag the box over the area. Choose the paper (A4, A3 or US Letter), which way up, and
   the scale (1:25,000 suits walking).
3. Press **Preview the page**, give it a title, and tick what to show: contours, routes,
   saved places, grid lines.
4. Press **Make the PDF**, then **Print**, or **Save as PDF** to keep the file.

Print at 100% (no "fit to page") so that the scale on the sheet is right. More in
[Printing a map](maps.md#printing-a-map).

## Check line of sight between two points

For a radio on VHF or above: can two places see each other over the ground?

1. Press **Line of sight between two points**.
2. Tap the map at the first end, then at the second. Pressing a saved place puts the
   end exactly on it.
3. Give the height of each aerial above the ground (1.5 m for a radio held in the hand)
   and the band.
4. Read the answer: **Clear**, **In sight, with the Fresnel zone partly blocked**,
   **Blocked**, or **Not known** where terrain is missing. The picture below shows the
   ground between the two.

If they cannot see each other, **Find high ground in sight of both** suggests places for
a relay. This is geometry over the terrain, not a promise that a signal will get through.

## See how far a radio could reach from a point

1. Press and hold the map at the spot (right-click with a mouse).
2. Choose **Estimated radio range from here**.
3. The ground in sight of an aerial there is shaded green, with rings at 10, 25, 50 and
   100 km. The card gives the share of ground in sight and the furthest point.

Under **Heights and details**, set your aerial's height and the other station's
(handheld, car or roof). Woods, buildings and power are not counted: radio often reaches
less than this.
