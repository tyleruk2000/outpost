# Tools: calculators

Six screens under **Tools** are small calculators: **Water**, **Rations**, **Outdoors**,
**Radio calculators**, **Building** and **Cooking**. Each input has its unit beside it, the
answer appears as you type, in figures and in a sentence, and **How this is worked out**
opens the formula, its source, the range it holds for and what it leaves out.

They need no server and store nothing on it. The unit and last figure in each input are
remembered on this device. **Copy** copies an answer; **Save as a page** makes a page in
Notes; the printable sheets (making water safe, cooking temperatures and sharing out a
stock) offer **Save as PDF**, **Add to library** and **Share**.

**General information, not advice.** Anything about health or safety is quoted from the
official body that publishes it, with the date read, and no more precisely than the source
gives it. Where bodies differ, each is quoted and the screen says whose figure it uses.

The screen notes where bodies differ: the EPA gives 5,000 ft as 1,000 m (it is about
1,524 m); the WHO and DWI give only a rolling boil; the CDC's pages disagree on chlorine
dioxide and Cryptosporidium; Ready.gov advises against tablets that are not plain bleach.

## Rations

How much energy a household needs a day, how long the food lasts, what to store, and how to
share a stock. **A planning aid, not medical or dietary advice.** Children, older people,
anyone pregnant, breastfeeding or ill should not be put on a reduced ration by a
calculator; their need is kept in full in every figure.

- **A day's energy.** List the household (or press **Use my household from Supplies**),
  giving anyone with heavy work, a baby or a doctor's figure their own. Choose UK,
  international or Sphere figures; the last two add an allowance below 20 °C.
- **How long food lasts.** Give kcal, kilograms of staples, or press **Use my supplies**:
  days at full need, and at three quarters and half for those who may be reduced, with
  protein, fat and a warning when one food is most of the energy.
- **Food to store.** Days and each staple's share: the weight of each to store.
- **Sharing out a stock.** A daily amount for each person by their need. Those on a full
  ration are given theirs first; the rest share what is left in proportion. Offered as a
  PDF.

fuel, vitamins and minerals, and anyone's own medical needs.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Antenna lengths | 0.95 × c ÷ 2f (half wave), ÷ 4f (quarter wave); factor adjustable | [ARRL](https://www.arrl.org/single-band-dipoles); [RSGB](https://rsgb.org/main/get-started-in-amateur-radio/antennas/your-first-antenna-the-half-wave-dipole/) | Cut long and trim to resonance in place. | Free-space path loss | 32.45 + 20 log f(MHz) + 20 log d(km) | [ITU-R P.525](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.525/en) | No ground, terrain or atmosphere; refused within one wavelength. With a power, gives the received level and S-meter reading. |
| Radio horizon | √(2kRh₁) + √(2kRh₂), k = 4/3 | [ITU-R P.834](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.834/en) | Smooth earth. The map's line of sight uses real terrain. |
| Cable loss | Read between the maker's published points | Times Microwave LMR-400 and LMR-240, SSB Ecoflex 10, Belden 8267, 9258, 8240; or your own figure | Refused outside the published frequencies. Leaves out SWR, connectors, water and age. |
| Watts, dBm and S-units | S9 = −73 dBm below 30 MHz, −93 dBm above; 6 dB a unit | [IARU Region 1 VHF Handbook v10.03](https://www.iaru-r1.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/VHF_Handbook_V10_03_final.pdf) | −93 dBm is confirmed for 144 MHz and up only. Real S-meters seldom follow it. |
| Battery life | capacity × usable share ÷ average draw over transmit, receive and standby | Arithmetic | The usable share (80%) is your working figure. For solar and several loads, use [Power and batteries](tools-power.md). |
